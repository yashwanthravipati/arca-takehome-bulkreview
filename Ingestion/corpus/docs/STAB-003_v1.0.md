# Stability Protocol Supporting Introduction of Alu/Alu Blister Packaging for Velorastat Film-Coated Tablets

**Document number:** STAB-003
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective date:** 2025-06-02
**Status:** Current
**Product:** Product A, Velorastat Film-Coated Tablets 25 mg, 50 mg and 100 mg
**Site:** Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals, Ridgeway Manufacturing and Quality Control Site

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This protocol defines the stability study that will generate data to support a variation introducing cold-form aluminium/aluminium (Alu/Alu) blister packaging for Velorastat Film-Coated Tablets. The tablets are currently registered in high-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles with a silica gel desiccant canister, and that presentation is supported by the registration data generated under STAB-001.

1.2 The study demonstrates that the Alu/Alu blister protects the product at least as well as the registered bottle presentation. It also provides the data needed to assign a shelf life and storage statement to the blister presentation. Data will be evaluated in accordance with ICH Q1A(R2) and ICH Q1E.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This protocol applies to Velorastat Film-Coated Tablets 25 mg and 100 mg packed in Alu/Alu blisters (10 tablets per card) at the Ridgeway site packaging hall, Line PK-04. The 50 mg strength falls within the scope of the variation through the bracketing design described in Section 10.

2.2 The drug product formulation, manufacturing process, manufacturing site and tablet film coat are unchanged from the registered product. The only change under evaluation is the primary container closure system.

2.3 The following are outside the scope of this protocol:

- the HDPE bottle presentation, which remains under STAB-001 for registration batches and STAB-002 for commercial batches;
- photostability testing, which was completed on unpackaged tablets under STAB-001 in accordance with ICH Q1B and showed that the tablets are not photosensitive;
- in-use stability, because blister units are single-dose and are protected until the point of administration.

## 4. Materials and batches

4.1 Three batches of each bracketing strength will be placed on study. Each batch was manufactured at commercial scale using the registered process and released against SPEC-A. Blister packaging was performed on Line PK-04 using production tooling.

4.2 The packaging components are:

- forming foil: OPA 25 µm / Al 45 µm / PVC 60 µm cold-form laminate, supplier code PKM-2210;
- lidding foil: 20 µm hard-temper aluminium with heat-seal lacquer, supplier code PKM-2214;
- cavity format: 25 mg uses tooling T-VLR-S (smallest cavity) and 100 mg uses tooling T-VLR-L (largest cavity). The 50 mg intermediate cavity, tooling T-VLR-M, is not packaged for this study.

## 5. Analytical methods

5.1 All testing will use the validated methods listed below. These are the same methods referenced in SPEC-A. Methods were validated in accordance with ICH Q2(R2) and are stability-indicating where applicable.

| Test | Method number | Principle | Tablets per test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appearance | AM-VLR-104 | Visual inspection of tablets and blister integrity | 20 |
| Assay | AM-VLR-101 | Reversed-phase HPLC, UV detection at 272 nm | 10 (composite) |
| Degradation products (VLR-ketone, unspecified, total) | AM-VLR-101 | Reversed-phase HPLC, gradient, UV at 272 nm; RRT of VLR-ketone approx. 1.34 | Same composite as assay |
| Dissolution | AM-VLR-102 | USP Apparatus 2, 50 rpm, 900 mL pH 6.8 phosphate buffer, 37 °C, HPLC finish | 12 (Stage 1 and Stage 2 reserve) |
| Water content | AM-VLR-103 | Coulometric Karl Fischer, oven transfer at 140 °C | 5 |
| Identification (initial only) | AM-VLR-101 | HPLC retention time match | Same composite as assay |
| Blister leak test (initial and 12 months only) | AM-PKG-011 | Methylene blue vacuum dye ingress | 10 cards |

5.2 Analysts will follow SOP-QC-008 (Stability Sample Testing and Data Recording). Chromatographic integration follows SOP-QC-019 (Chromatographic Data Processing). Impurity results are reported to two decimal places and rounded according to SPEC-A.

## 6. Storage conditions

6.1 The registered bottle presentation is stored for long-term testing at 30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH. The same climatic zone condition applies to the blister presentation, because the product is intended for supply to Zone IVa markets as well as Zones I and II. An intermediate condition is not included. Under ICH Q1A(R2), intermediate testing is required only where the long-term condition is 25°C / 60% RH and a significant change occurs at accelerated, and neither applies here.

6.2 Blister samples will be stored at 30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH for long-term testing and at 40°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH for accelerated testing. The long-term samples will be stored in walk-in chamber SC-07 and the accelerated samples in reach-in chamber SC-11. Both chambers are qualified and mapped under SOP-ENG-032, with continuous monitoring and alarm notification through the site environmental monitoring system (EMS ID: RIDGE-EMS-02).

| Chamber | Condition | Location | Last mapping | Alarm set points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SC-07 | Long-term | Building 4, Room 4.112 | 2025-01-20 | ±1.5 °C / ±4% RH warning; ±2 °C / ±5% RH action |
| SC-11 | Accelerated | Building 4, Room 4.115 | 2025-02-03 | ±1.5 °C / ±4% RH warning; ±2 °C / ±5% RH action |

6.3 Samples will be placed on study within 30 days of packaging. Day 0 is defined as the date the samples are placed in the chamber. Before placement, cartons are held in the controlled ambient sample store at 15–25 °C.

## 7. Testing schedule

7.1 Blister samples will be pulled at 0, 3, 6, 9 and 12 months at the long-term condition and at 0, 1, 3 and 6 months at the accelerated condition. The initial (0 month) results are generated once per batch and serve both conditions. Pull windows are ±3 days for the 1-month accelerated pull, ±7 days for the 3-month pulls and ±14 days for all later pulls. Testing must start within 14 days of the pull date. Pulled samples are held at 2–8 °C pending analysis, as permitted by SOP-QC-008.

7.2 Sample allocation per batch per pull point is as follows:

|---|---|

7.3 Each batch has 7 scheduled post-initial pulls and 1 initial pull, using 80 cards. A further 10 cards are allocated for the leak tests, and 50 cards are held as contingency for extended or additional time points. Contingency cards are stored at the long-term condition. This gives the 140 cards per batch shown in Section 4.

7.4 If a significant change, as defined in ICH Q1A(R2), occurs at the accelerated condition, Regulatory Affairs and QA will assess the impact on the proposed shelf life. The long-term study will continue unaffected.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

8.1 Acceptance criteria are the shelf-life limits in SPEC-A. Release limits in SPEC-A are tighter for assay (97.0–103.0% of label claim). Release limits are not applied to stability results.

8.2 The blister-packaged tablets must remain within assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, VLR-ketone not more than 0.3%, total impurities not more than 1.0%, and dissolution not less than 80% (Q) in 30 minutes for the duration of the study. The additional attributes listed below are monitored for information and trending. They are also used to confirm that the blister barrier performs equivalently to the desiccated bottle.

| Test | Acceptance criterion (shelf life) |
|---|---|
| Appearance | Yellow, oval, biconvex film-coated tablets debossed per SPEC-A; blisters intact |
| Assay | 95.0–105.0% of label claim |
| VLR-ketone | NMT 0.3% |
| Any unspecified degradation product | NMT 0.2% |
| Total impurities | NMT 1.0% |
| Dissolution | NLT 80% (Q) in 30 minutes |
| Water content | Report result (for trending; bottle presentation historical range 2.1–3.4% w/w) |
| Blister leak test | No dye ingress |

8.3 Dissolution results are evaluated using the USP <711> acceptance table at Stages S1 to S3. The Stage 2 reserve tablets are drawn from the 2 allocated cards. Stage 3 tablets, if needed, are drawn from the reserve cards.

## 9. Deviation handling

9.1 Any departure from this protocol is recorded and investigated under SOP-QA-014 (Deviation Management). Examples include a missed pull window, a sample mix-up, a test performed with a non-specified method or a chamber excursion. Out-of-specification results are investigated under SOP-QC-021 (Investigation of OOS Results). Out-of-trend results are evaluated against the trending rules in SOP-QC-024 (Stability Data Trending).

<!-- page 5 -->

9.2 Chamber excursions beyond the action limits are handled under SOP-QC-037 (Stability Chamber Excursion Management). The EMS record is reviewed, the cumulative time out of range is calculated and the impact on samples is assessed. Excursions shorter than 24 hours that remain within ±5 °C and ±10% RH are normally considered to have no impact. However, every excursion is documented, and the impact assessment is approved by QA.

9.3 All deviations, together with their root cause, impact assessment and CAPA references, will be summarised in the interim and final stability reports.

9.4 No deviations have been recorded under this protocol as of the date of this version.

## 10. Bracketing and matrixing

10.1 A bracketing design in accordance with ICH Q1D is applied across strengths. All three strengths are compressed from a common blend with proportional composition. All three use the same film coat and are packed in the same Alu/Alu laminate. Only the tablet size and blister cavity size differ between strengths.

10.2 Only the 25 mg and 100 mg strengths are packaged in blisters for this study because they represent the smallest and largest blister cavities, which bracket the 50 mg presentation. The 50 mg tablet and tooling T-VLR-M are intermediate in both tablet dimensions and cavity headspace volume. The ratio of foil surface area to tablet mass for the 50 mg strength therefore lies between those of the extremes. The stability of the 50 mg blister presentation is inferred from the bracketing strengths. If either extreme shows behaviour that differs materially from the bottle data, the 50 mg strength will be placed on study by protocol revision.

10.3 Matrixing is not applied. Every batch is tested for every attribute at every scheduled time point, as shown in Section 7.

## 11. Post-approval commitments

11.1 Following approval of the blister presentation, the first three commercial blister batches will be continued at the long-term condition through 36 months and the data will be submitted in a post-approval variation. These batches will be tested at 18, 24 and 36 months, in addition to the time points defined in Section 7, using the methods and acceptance criteria of this protocol.

11.2 After the commitment batches, at least one blister batch per strength packaged per year will be enrolled in the ongoing stability programme defined in STAB-002.

11.3 The shelf life proposed in the initial variation will be supported by the 12-month long-term and 6-month accelerated data. Any extrapolation will follow ICH Q1E. Any extension beyond the initially approved shelf life will be supported by the commitment data described in 11.1.
