# Drug Product Specification: Zentoprazine Extended-Release Tablets 50 mg, 100 mg and 200 mg

**Document number:** SPEC-E
**Version:** 3.0
**Effective date:** 2025-03-27
**Status:** Current
**Product:** Product E, Zentoprazine Extended-Release Tablets, 50 mg, 100 mg and 200 mg, in HDPE bottles
**Document owner:** Regulatory Affairs CMC, Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This specification defines the tests, analytical procedures and acceptance criteria that Zentoprazine Extended-Release Tablets 50 mg, 100 mg and 200 mg must meet. Batches are tested against these criteria at release and throughout the approved shelf life.

1.2 This document is the controlled reference for batch disposition by Quality Assurance. It is also the reference for stability testing under the protocols listed in Section 8, for regulatory submissions, and for out-of-specification (OOS) investigations conducted under SOP-QC-0412, Investigation of Out-of-Specification and Out-of-Trend Laboratory Results.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This specification applies to all commercial and registration batches of Zentoprazine Extended-Release Tablets 50 mg, 100 mg and 200 mg packaged in high-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles. It covers batches manufactured at the Aldencourt Ashby Vale manufacturing facility (Building 4, Oral Solid Dose) and at the Lindgren site.

2.2 Release testing is performed by Aldencourt Quality Control, Ashby Vale, or by the Lindgren site QC laboratory for batches manufactured there. In both cases testing uses the analytical procedures listed in Section 6, which have been transferred and qualified at both laboratories under SOP-QC-0230, Analytical Method Transfer.

2.3 Shelf-life acceptance criteria apply to stability samples tested under STAB-013, STAB-014 and STAB-015. They also apply to any market complaint or retained sample testing performed during the labelled shelf life of 36 months when stored at or below 25 °C.

2.4 This specification does not cover the drug substance, which is controlled separately under the drug substance specification maintained by Supplier Quality. It also does not cover in-process controls, which are defined in the master batch records MBR-E-050, MBR-E-100 and MBR-E-200.

## 3. Product description and composition

3.1 Zentoprazine Extended-Release Tablets are hydrophilic matrix tablets containing zentoprazine hydrochloride equivalent to 50 mg, 100 mg or 200 mg of zentoprazine free base. Drug release is controlled by hypromellose (HPMC K100M). The core is film-coated with a non-functional colour coat that provides strength differentiation and does not contribute to release control.

## 4. Specification table (release and shelf-life criteria, with method references)

The column headed "Shelf-life" applies to stability samples and to any testing during the labelled shelf life. Where the two columns give the same value, the limit is identical at release and through shelf life.

4.2 Zentoprazine extended-release tablets shall comply with assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, ZTP N-oxide not more than 0.3%, total impurities not more than 1.2%, and drug release of 15–35% at 2 hours, 50–75% at 8 hours and not less than 85% at 16 hours. ## 5. Justification of specification

5.1 **General.** The specification was established in line with ICH Q6A, including Decision Trees #1, #2 and #7. It is based on development data and on registration batch data. These comprise three batches per strength manufactured at Ashby Vale (batches E050-R01 to E050-R03, E100-R01 to E100-R03 and E200-R01 to E200-R03) and placed on stability under STAB-013. Supporting data come from three Lindgren site batches (L-E100-01, L-E100-02 and L-E200-01) placed on stability under STAB-014.

5.2 **Assay.** The 95.0–105.0% limit applies both at release and through shelf life. Registration batches released between 98.6% and 101.4% of label claim. The maximum decline observed after 24 months at 25 °C/60% RH was 1.3% absolute. After 6 months at 40 °C/75% RH, the maximum decline was 2.1% absolute. Statistical extrapolation per ICH Q1E supports retention within 95.0–105.0% over 36 months. A separate, tighter release limit was therefore not considered necessary.

5.3 **ZTP N-oxide.** ZTP N-oxide is the principal oxidative degradant. It forms in the presence of peroxide residues in povidone and hypromellose and is controlled by excipient peroxide limits in the excipient specifications. The maximum daily dose is 400 mg, so under ICH Q3B(R2) the reporting threshold is 0.1%, the identification threshold is 0.2% and the qualification threshold is 0.2%.

ZTP N-oxide was qualified at levels up to 0.5% in a 13-week oral repeat-dose toxicity study in rats (Study TX-ZTP-0219). It was negative in the bacterial reverse mutation assay (Study GT-ZTP-0107). The highest level observed in registration stability batches was 0.18% at 24 months at 25 °C/60% RH and 0.26% at 6 months at 40 °C/75% RH. Projected 36-month values remain below 0.25%. A limit of NMT 0.3% therefore applies at release and through shelf life. It is supported by the toxicological qualification and accommodates expected growth.

<!-- page 5 -->

5.4 **Desmethyl-zentoprazine (ZTP-DM).** ZTP-DM is a process-related impurity carried over from the drug substance. It increases slowly on storage. It is controlled below the ICH Q3B(R2) qualification threshold of 0.2% (shelf life). A tighter release limit of 0.15% assures compliance at end of shelf life.

5.5 **Unspecified degradation products.** These are limited to the identification threshold (NMT 0.20% at shelf life). A release limit of NMT 0.10% reflects the absence of any unspecified peak above 0.07% in any registration or Lindgren batch at release.

5.6 **Total impurities.** The NMT 1.2% limit applies at release and through shelf life. Total impurities in registration batches ranged from 0.31% to 0.44% at release and reached a maximum of 0.71% at 24 months under long-term conditions. The limit allows for ZTP N-oxide at its specified maximum plus ZTP-DM and unspecified impurities. It retains an appropriate margin and is consistent with the totals seen in the stressed samples supporting AM-E-102 validation.

5.7 **Drug release.** The three-point release profile follows ICH Q6A Decision Tree #7 for extended-release products: an early time point to exclude dose dumping, an intermediate point to define the profile shape, and a late point to confirm essentially complete release. The limits are 15–35% at 2 hours, 50–75% at 8 hours and NLT 85% at 16 hours. They were set from the mean profiles of the pivotal clinical batches (E100-C02 and E200-C01). Those batches released 24%, 63% and 94% at 2, 8 and 16 hours respectively. The ranges do not exceed ±10% of the clinical batch means, in accordance with regional guidance on extended-release dosage forms.

Dissolution is performed in 900 mL of pH 6.8 phosphate buffer at 37.0 ± 0.5 °C using USP Apparatus 1 (baskets) at 100 rpm. Development data in pH 1.2 and pH 4.5 media and in 40% ethanolic medium are held in the development report DR-ZTP-031. These conditions are not part of routine testing.

5.8 **Water content.** Water uptake by the hydrophilic matrix was shown during development to slow drug release at 2 hours. Water is therefore controlled at NMT 3.0% w/w at release and NMT 4.0% w/w through shelf life. The highest value observed in desiccated HDPE bottles at 24 months was 3.4%.

5.9 **Uniformity of dosage units.** Content uniformity is required because the drug load is below 25% w/w in the 50 mg strength. It is applied to all strengths for consistency.

5.10 **Residual solvents and microbial limits.** Ethanol is the only Class 3 solvent used, in granulation. It is controlled at release only, because it cannot increase on storage. Microbial limits follow USP <1111> for non-aqueous oral preparations. Because water activity is low, microbial testing on stability is reduced to annual and end-of-shelf-life time points.

<!-- page 6 -->

5.11 **Stability programs.** Ongoing commitment batches are tested under the matrixing design in STAB-015. That design brackets the 30-count 50 mg and 200 mg presentations and matrixes intermediate time points. All shelf-life criteria in Section 4 apply equally to time points tested under STAB-015.

## 6. Analytical procedures

6.1 All analytical procedures are validated in accordance with ICH Q2(R2). Summary validation data are held in the corresponding validation reports referenced below. Each method is executed by trained analysts per SOP-QC-0105, Analyst Training and Qualification.

| Method | Title | Technique summary | Validation report |
|---|---|---|---|
| AM-E-100 | Visual Inspection of Zentoprazine ER Tablets | Visual examination under diffuse white light, 1000–1500 lux | VR-E-100 |
| AM-E-101 | Assay and Identification of Zentoprazine ER Tablets by HPLC | RP-HPLC, C18 150 × 4.6 mm, 3.5 µm; gradient phosphate buffer pH 3.0/acetonitrile; UV 254 nm with DAD | VR-E-101 |
| AM-E-102 | Related Substances of Zentoprazine ER Tablets by HPLC | RP-HPLC, C18 250 × 4.6 mm, 5 µm; 55-minute gradient; UV 230 nm; LOQ 0.03% | VR-E-102 |
| AM-E-103 | Content Uniformity of Zentoprazine ER Tablets | Single-tablet extraction, HPLC per AM-E-101 conditions | VR-E-103 |
| AM-E-104 | Drug Release of Zentoprazine ER Tablets | USP Apparatus 1, 100 rpm, 900 mL pH 6.8 phosphate buffer, 37.0 ± 0.5 °C; online UV at 275 nm with 1 cm cell; sampling at 2, 8 and 16 h (plus 1, 4 and 12 h for profile reporting where required) | VR-E-104 |
| AM-E-105 | Water Content by Karl Fischer Titration | Volumetric KF, methanol/formamide 2:1 | VR-E-105 |
| AM-E-106 | Residual Solvents by Headspace GC | HS-GC-FID, DB-624 column | VR-E-106 |
| AM-E-107 | Microbial Enumeration and Specified Microorganisms | USP <61>/<62>, membrane filtration | VR-E-107 |

6.2 AM-E-102 was validated as stability-indicating. Forced degradation covered acid, base, peroxide, heat, humidity and ICH Q1B photolysis. All degradants were resolved from zentoprazine and from each other (resolution ≥ 2.0). Peak purity of the principal peak was confirmed by DAD. ZTP N-oxide elutes at a relative retention time of approximately 0.62 and ZTP-DM at approximately 0.88.

6.3 For AM-E-104, the 1, 4 and 12 hour sampling points are reported for information on registration and Lindgren comparability batches only. Acceptance is assessed solely at 2, 8 and 16 hours.

6.4 System suitability criteria are defined in each method and must be met before results are reported. Any failure of system suitability invalidates the run and is documented per SOP-QC-0318, Laboratory Event Management.
