# Stability Protocol for Tirexafen Oral Suspension Manufactured at the Riverton Site

**Document number:** STAB-005
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective date:** 2025-01-27
**Status:** Current
**Product:** Product B, Tirexafen Oral Suspension 40 mg/mL, 150 mL amber glass bottle
**Document type:** Stability protocol
**Owner:** Regulatory Affairs, Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines the design, conduct and evaluation of the stability study for Tirexafen Oral Suspension 40 mg/mL manufactured at the Aldencourt Riverton site. The study supports the transfer of commercial manufacture from the Hallbrook site, where the registration batches described in STAB-004 were produced, to the Riverton site. Its objective is to show that drug product manufactured at Riverton has a stability profile comparable to that of the registration batches and complies with the approved shelf-life acceptance criteria. The data generated under this protocol will be included in the site-transfer variation submission and will be evaluated in accordance with ICH Q1A(R2) and ICH Q1E.

## 2. Scope

This protocol applies to Tirexafen Oral Suspension 40 mg/mL filled into 150 mL Type III amber glass bottles with 28 mm child-resistant polypropylene closures and LDPE liners, manufactured, filled and packaged at the Riverton site. It covers the three site-transfer batches listed in Section 4. It also describes the post-approval stability commitment for the first commercial batches produced at Riverton.

The following are outside the scope of this protocol:

- registration stability of Hallbrook-manufactured batches, which is governed by STAB-004;
- in-use stability after first opening of the bottle, which is governed by STAB-006;
- photostability testing, which was completed on the registration batches under STAB-004 in accordance with ICH Q1B and is not repeated here, because the formulation and the amber primary container are unchanged;
- ongoing annual stability monitoring of routine commercial batches, which is managed under SOP-QA-0412.

## 4. Materials and batches

### 4.1 Batches

Three consecutive process performance qualification (PPQ) batches manufactured at Riverton will be placed on study. Each batch is manufactured at the full commercial scale of 600 L, which corresponds to approximately 3,900 bottles of 150 mL nominal fill. Each batch uses a different lot of Tirexafen drug substance.

| Batch number | Manufacturing date | Batch size | Drug substance lot | Fill volume (nominal) | Packaging lot (bottles / closures) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RV-TXF-24-001 | 2024-11-18 | 600 L | TXF-API-2408-031 | 150 mL | AGB-150-24-117 / CRC-28-24-062 |
| RV-TXF-24-002 | 2024-11-25 | 600 L | TXF-API-2409-007 | 150 mL | AGB-150-24-117 / CRC-28-24-062 |
| RV-TXF-24-003 | 2024-12-02 | 600 L | TXF-API-2410-014 | 150 mL | AGB-150-24-121 / CRC-28-24-066 |

All three batches must be released against SPEC-B before they are placed on stability. The release certificates of analysis are filed in the stability study file STF-005.

### 4.2 Container closure and orientation

Samples are stored in the commercial container closure system, labelled, but without carton and patient leaflet. Each bottle carries a stability label that shows the protocol number, batch number, storage condition, orientation and pull time point. For each batch and condition, half of the bottles are stored upright and half inverted. Inverted storage provides worst-case contact between the suspension and the closure liner.

### 4.3 Sample quantities

The bottle quantities per batch are shown below. For each time point and condition, 4 bottles are allocated for testing and 2 bottles are held in reserve.

| Condition | Time points | Bottles per time point (test + reserve) | Bottles per batch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial (shared) | 0 months | 4 + 2 | 6 |
| Long-term | 3, 6, 9, 12 months | 4 + 2 | 24 |
| Accelerated | 3, 6 months | 4 + 2 | 12 |
| Contingency reserve | n/a | 6 | 6 |
| **Total per batch** | | | **48** |

For the three batches, 144 bottles are required in total. Bottles are allocated as follows: 2 bottles for assay and impurities, 1 bottle for pH, viscosity and appearance, and 1 bottle for preservative content and microbial enumeration. The initial (0 month) result for each batch serves as the starting point for both storage conditions.

## 5. Analytical methods

All methods were transferred from Hallbrook to Riverton under method transfer protocol MTP-TXF-003, and the transfer report MTR-TXF-003 has been approved. The methods are validated in accordance with ICH Q2(R2).

<!-- page 3 -->

| Test | Method number | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance and resuspendability | AM-TXF-001 | Visual; inversion count to uniform suspension |
| Assay (Tirexafen) | AM-TXF-012 | Reversed-phase HPLC-UV, 254 nm |
| Related substances (TXF-acid, individual and total impurities) | AM-TXF-013 | Gradient reversed-phase HPLC-UV, 230 nm |
| pH | AM-GEN-003 | Potentiometry at 25°C |
| Viscosity | AM-TXF-020 | Rotational viscometer, spindle LV-2, 30 rpm |
| Preservative content (sodium benzoate) | AM-TXF-015 | Isocratic HPLC-UV |
| Microbial enumeration | AM-MIC-008 | Ph. Eur. 2.6.12 / USP <61> |

TXF-acid is the hydrolytic degradation product of Tirexafen and the principal degradant seen in the registration studies. It is reported as a percentage relative to the Tirexafen peak, using a relative response factor of 1.12 as established in the validation report for AM-TXF-013.

## 6. Storage conditions

Batches manufactured at the Riverton site will be stored at 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH and at 40°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH. The first condition is the long-term condition and the second is the accelerated condition. They are consistent with ICH Q1A(R2) for products intended for climatic zones I and II.

| Condition | Chamber ID | Location | Qualification report |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-term, 25°C / 60% RH | RIV-SC-07 | Building 4, Stability Suite B | EQR-SC-07-2024 |
| Accelerated, 40°C / 75% RH | RIV-SC-09 | Building 4, Stability Suite B | EQR-SC-09-2024 |
| Backup (long-term) | RIV-SC-08 | Building 4, Stability Suite B | EQR-SC-08-2024 |

The intermediate condition of 30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH was included in the registration study under STAB-004 and showed no significant change through 12 months. It is not part of this protocol. If a significant change, as defined in ICH Q1A(R2), occurs at the accelerated condition, the matter will be handled under Section 9. Any additional testing will then be defined in a protocol amendment.

Chamber temperature and humidity are recorded continuously by the validated environmental monitoring system (EMS) at 5-minute intervals. Alarm limits are set inside the ICH tolerances: ±1.5°C and ±4% RH, with a 15-minute alarm delay. If a chamber fails, samples are moved to the qualified backup chamber in accordance with SOP-ENG-0220. Excursions are assessed in accordance with Section 9.

## 7. Testing schedule

Samples will be withdrawn at 0, 3, 6, 9 and 12 months from long-term storage and at 0, 3 and 6 months from accelerated storage. Time zero is the date on which the samples are placed in the chambers, which must be no later than 30 days after the manufacturing date. Pulls must take place within ±7 days of the scheduled date for the 3, 6 and 9 month time points and within ±14 days for the 12 month time point. Testing must start within 14 days of the pull. Samples waiting for testing are held at 2–8°C.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

For this site-transfer study, samples must comply with assay 90.0–110.0% of label claim, TXF-acid not more than 0.5%, total impurities not more than 1.5%, and pH between 4.5 and 5.5. These are the shelf-life acceptance criteria and apply to every time point under both storage conditions.

The release limits in SPEC-B are tighter for some attributes. For example, the release limit for assay is 95.0–105.0% of label claim. Release limits are not used as stability acceptance criteria under this protocol. However, a result that falls outside a release limit while remaining within the shelf-life criteria will be evaluated as a potential out-of-trend result.

The following attributes are reported for information and trending and are compared against the corresponding SPEC-B values:

- appearance;
- resuspendability;
- viscosity;
- preservative content;
- microbial enumeration;
- individual unspecified impurities.

These attributes are not acceptance criteria for the purposes of this protocol. If an informational result falls outside the SPEC-B value, it is handled as an OOT event under Section 9.

Results will be evaluated in accordance with ICH Q1E. For assay, TXF-acid and total impurities, the Riverton results will be compared statistically with the STAB-004 registration data, using regression analysis of slopes with a poolability test at α = 0.25. A Riverton batch is considered comparable if its slopes do not differ significantly from those of the registration batches and all results comply with the criteria in this section.

## 9. Deviation handling

Any departure from this protocol, or any unexpected event that may affect the integrity of the study, must be recorded as a deviation in the electronic quality management system (QMS) in accordance with SOP-QA-0105. This includes, but is not limited to:

- missed or late pulls;
- chamber excursions outside the ICH tolerances;
- sample mix-ups;
- testing outside the defined window;
- use of non-qualified equipment.

Chamber excursions are assessed in accordance with SOP-ENG-0220. Excursions shorter than 24 hours that remain within ±5°C and ±10% RH may be closed with a documented impact assessment. Longer or larger excursions require a QA-approved investigation and an assessment of whether reserve samples, or a repeat of the affected time point, are needed.

OOS results are investigated in accordance with SOP-QC-0301. OOT results, defined as a value outside the 95% prediction interval of the regression for that batch and attribute, are investigated in accordance with SOP-QC-0308. Confirmed OOS results under long-term storage are escalated to the Head of Quality within 2 working days and assessed for regulatory reporting.

Each deviation, together with its root cause, corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) and impact on the study conclusions, is summarized in the final stability report. No deviations have been recorded under this protocol at version 1.0.

## 10. Bracketing and matrixing

Bracketing is not used because only one strength and one container size are manufactured at the Riverton site. The 40 mg/mL suspension is the only strength in the Riverton product portfolio, and the 150 mL amber glass bottle is the only presentation filled at Riverton. The 60 mL presentation registered under STAB-004 continues to be filled only at Hallbrook.

Matrixing is not used either. All batches are tested at all scheduled time points for all attributes listed in Section 7. Full testing is considered appropriate because the study involves only three batches and a single presentation, and because a complete data set is needed to support the site-transfer comparison. This approach is consistent with ICH Q1D.

## 11. Post-approval commitments

The first three commercial batches produced at the Riverton site will be placed on long-term stability through 24 months, with results provided in the annual report. These batches will be stored at the long-term condition defined in Section 6 and tested for the attributes listed in Section 7. After 12 months, the batches will be pulled at the extended time points of the long-term schedule in STAB-004.

<!-- page 6 -->

The commitment batches are separate from the three PPQ batches in Section 4. They will be identified in the stability study file STF-005 when they are manufactured. Regulatory Affairs will track progress against this commitment in the commitments register RA-CR-2025.

Any confirmed OOS result in a commitment batch will be reported to the competent authorities within the timelines set out in SOP-RA-0210. Reporting will not wait for the annual report.
