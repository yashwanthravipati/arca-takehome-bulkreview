# Registration Stability Protocol for Moxilurine 150 mg Hard Capsules (Climatic Zones I to IVb)

**Document number:** STAB-010
**Version:** 2.0
**Effective date:** 2023-11-06
**Status:** Current
**Product:** Product D, Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules (HPMC shells)
**Owner:** Regulatory Affairs, Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals, Hollins Park Site

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This protocol defines the design, conduct and evaluation of the registration stability study for Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules. The data generated under this protocol will support the shelf life and storage statements proposed in marketing authorisation applications in all climatic zones, including hot and very humid (Zone IVb) territories.

1.2 The study is designed in accordance with ICH Q1A(R2), with reduced designs justified under ICH Q1D and data evaluation performed according to ICH Q1E. The study establishes a re-test-independent shelf life for the drug product in each commercial container closure system and identifies any trends in quality attributes that could affect safety or efficacy over the proposed shelf life.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This protocol applies to the three primary stability batches of Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules manufactured at the Aldencourt Hollins Park site (Building 4, Oral Solid Dose Suite B) using HPMC capsule shells from the qualified primary supplier.

2.2 The following presentations are within scope:

- PVC/PVDC (250 µm / 90 g/m²) blister with 20 µm hard-temper aluminium lidding foil, 10 capsules per card;
- 30-count white HDPE bottle (60 mL) with induction-sealed polypropylene child-resistant closure and 1 g silica gel canister;
- 60-count white HDPE bottle (100 mL), same closure and desiccant system;
- 90-count white HDPE bottle (150 mL), same closure and desiccant system.

2.3 Capsules filled into HPMC shells sourced from the alternate supplier are excluded from this protocol and are covered by STAB-011. Commercial batches placed on annual stability after approval are covered by STAB-012. The drug product release and shelf-life specification is SPEC-D.

## 3. Responsibilities

3.1 **Regulatory Affairs (RA):** authors and maintains this protocol, ensures alignment with registration strategy and current ICH guidance, and compiles stability data summaries for Module 3.2.P.8 of the dossier.

3.2 **Quality Control Stability Group (QC-STB):** places samples on station, manages chamber inventory, executes pulls within the defined windows, performs testing according to the referenced analytical methods, and records results in the laboratory information management system (LIMS, study code STB-D-010).

3.3 **Quality Assurance (QA):** approves the protocol and any amendments, reviews and approves stability data, oversees deviation and out-of-specification (OOS) investigations, and confirms that study conduct conforms to GMP.

<!-- page 2 -->

3.4 **Engineering and Facilities:** qualify, maintain and monitor the stability chambers and associated alarm systems in accordance with SOP-ENG-207 and SOP-QC-214.

3.5 **Pharmaceutical Development:** provides scientific input on trend evaluation, statistical analysis of shelf life under ICH Q1E, and assessment of any significant change.

## 5. Analytical methods

5.1 All methods are validated in accordance with ICH Q2(R1) and are stability-indicating where applicable. Forced degradation studies (acid, base, oxidative, thermal, humidity and photolytic stress) demonstrated that MXL-dimer and the other known degradation products are resolved from the Moxilurine peak and from each other.

<!-- page 3 -->

**Table 3. Analytical methods**

| Attribute | Method number | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance and description | AM-D-100 | Visual examination against SPEC-D description |
| Assay | AM-D-101 | Reversed-phase HPLC, UV 254 nm |
| Related substances (MXL-dimer, MXL-N-oxide, unspecified, total) | AM-D-101 | Reversed-phase HPLC, gradient, UV 254 nm |
| Dissolution | AM-D-102 | USP <711> Apparatus 1 (basket), 100 rpm, 900 mL phosphate buffer pH 6.8, HPLC finish |
| Water content | AM-D-103 | Karl Fischer coulometric titration |
| Identification | AM-D-104 | HPLC retention time and UV spectrum (initial only) |
| Microbial enumeration and specified organisms | AM-D-105 | Ph. Eur. 2.6.12 / 2.6.13 |

5.2 Dissolution is performed in phosphate buffer pH 6.8 rather than 0.1 N HCl because the HPMC shell opening is independent of pH in this medium and the method demonstrated superior discrimination for capsule ageing during development.

5.3 Any change to a referenced method during the study shall be managed through change control under SOP-QA-019 and supported by a method bridging assessment.

## 6. Storage conditions

6.1 To support global registration including Zone IVb markets, long-term storage will be at 30°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH and accelerated storage at 40°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH. Because the long-term condition is already 30°C / 75% RH, an intermediate condition of 30°C / 65% RH is not included; data at 30°C / 75% RH are considered to bound storage in Zones I, II, III and IVa.

6.2 Samples are stored in the following qualified walk-in chambers. Chamber qualification, mapping and continuous monitoring are performed in accordance with SOP-ENG-207 and SOP-QC-214.

**Table 4. Stability chambers**

| Condition | Primary chamber | Backup chamber | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-term, 30°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH | SC-07 | SC-11 | Building 6, Stability Hall 1 |
| Accelerated, 40°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH | SC-09 | SC-12 | Building 6, Stability Hall 1 |

6.3 Samples are stored upright in their final packaging. Blister cards are placed in their commercial cartons. Bottles are stored with closures fully applied and induction seals intact.

6.4 A separate photostability study on batch D23-0412 (unpackaged capsules, immediate pack and carton) is performed according to ICH Q1B Option 2 under SOP-QC-236. Results are reported with this study but the samples are not part of the time-point schedule in Section 7.

<!-- page 4 -->

6.5 Chamber excursions are assessed according to SOP-QC-214. Excursions shorter than 24 hours and within ± 4°C or ± 10% RH of set point are documented and evaluated by QC-STB; excursions outside these limits are handled as deviations under Section 9.

## 7. Testing schedule

7.1 Time zero is defined as the date of placement on station. Initial (T0) results may be taken from release testing if release was completed within 30 days before placement; otherwise full T0 testing is performed. Long-term pulls are scheduled at 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24 and 36 months, with accelerated pulls at 0, 3 and 6 months.

7.2 Pull windows are ± 7 days for time points up to and including 12 months and ± 14 days for later time points. Testing must begin within 14 days of the pull. Samples pulled and awaiting test are stored at 2–8°C under SOP-QC-221.

7.3 Table 5 applies in full to the long-term condition. At the accelerated condition, appearance, assay, related substances, dissolution and water content are tested at each scheduled pull; microbial enumeration is not performed.

7.4 If significant change, as defined in ICH Q1A(R2) Section 2.2.7.1, occurs at the accelerated condition, Pharmaceutical Development and RA will assess the impact on the proposed storage statement. Because the long-term condition is 30°C / 75% RH, no intermediate testing is triggered.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

8.1 Acceptance criteria are taken from the shelf-life limits of SPEC-D and are applied to each batch, presentation, condition and time point. Capsules shall meet assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, MXL-dimer not more than 0.2%, total impurities not more than 1.0%, and dissolution not less than 75% (Q) in 45 minutes at each pull.

<!-- page 5 -->

**Table 6. Shelf-life acceptance criteria applied in this study**

| Attribute | Acceptance criterion |
|---|---|
| Appearance | Size 1 white opaque HPMC capsule, printed "AD 150" in black; contents white to off-white powder |
| Assay | 95.0–105.0% of label claim |
| MXL-dimer | NMT 0.2% |
| MXL-N-oxide | NMT 0.3% |
| Any unspecified impurity | NMT 0.10% |
| Total impurities | NMT 1.0% |
| Dissolution | NLT 75% (Q) in 45 minutes |
| Water content | NMT 7.0% w/w |
| TAMC / TYMC | NMT 10³ CFU/g / NMT 10² CFU/g |
| *E. coli* | Absent in 1 g |

8.2 Impurities are reported to two decimal places at or above the reporting threshold of 0.05%, consistent with ICH Q3B(R2). Water content is reported for information on trend; the HPMC shell is expected to show lower moisture uptake than gelatin and no limit below 7.0% w/w is applied.

8.3 Shelf-life estimation will be performed according to ICH Q1E using regression analysis at the 95% one-sided confidence limit for assay, MXL-dimer, total impurities and dissolution. Poolability of batches will be tested at a significance level of 0.25.

## 9. Deviation handling

9.1 Any departure from this protocol, including missed or late pulls, chamber excursions outside the limits in 6.5, sample mix-ups, and use of unvalidated or superseded methods, shall be recorded as a deviation in the QMS under SOP-QA-031. QA shall classify each deviation as minor, major or critical and approve the impact assessment and corrective and preventive actions (CAPA).

9.2 Any result outside an acceptance criterion in Section 8 shall be investigated as a suspected OOS result under SOP-QC-118. Phase I (laboratory) investigation is completed within 5 working days; if no assignable laboratory cause is identified, a Phase II (full-scale) investigation is opened under QA oversight. Results that are within specification but outside the predicted trend are handled as out-of-trend (OOT) results under SOP-QC-119.

9.3 A confirmed OOS result is escalated to the Head of Quality and RA within one working day of confirmation so that regulatory reporting obligations can be assessed.

9.4 No deviations, OOS results or OOT results have been recorded under this protocol as of the effective date of version 2.0. All chamber monitoring records for SC-07 and SC-09 since placement are within the qualified ranges.

## 10. Bracketing and matrixing

10.1 Bracketing is applied to the HDPE bottle presentations by testing the 30-count and 90-count bottles, which represent the extremes of fill and headspace for the 60-count bottle. All three bottle sizes share the same resin grade, wall thickness specification, closure, induction seal and desiccant canister, and differ only in bottle volume and capsule count. The ratio of headspace to capsule mass, and therefore the moisture and oxygen challenge per capsule, is greatest in the 30-count bottle and smallest in the 90-count bottle. The 60-count bottle lies between these extremes.

<!-- page 6 -->

10.2 The 60-count bottles from each primary batch are placed on station at both conditions in quantities sufficient for the full schedule. They are not tested unless:

- a result for either bracketing presentation fails an acceptance criterion or shows an OOT result; or
- the 30-count and 90-count presentations show trends that diverge sufficiently that the intermediate presentation can no longer be assumed to be bounded.

In either case, the 60-count samples at the corresponding and all subsequent time points will be tested, and RA will assess whether the bracketing design remains valid under ICH Q1D.

10.3 The PVC/PVDC blister is a different container closure system and is tested in full at all time points and conditions.

10.4 Matrixing is not applied. All batches and all tested presentations are tested at every scheduled time point.

## 11. Post-approval commitments

11.1 At the time of submission the dossier is expected to contain at least 12 months of long-term data and 6 months of accelerated data on the three primary batches, supporting a proposed initial shelf life of 24 months with the storage statement "Do not store above 30°C. Store in the original package in order to protect from moisture."

11.2 The applicant commits to completing the 36-month long-term study on the three primary batches and to notifying the agency of any confirmed out-of-specification result. Stability data will be submitted as they become available according to the variation procedures of each region, and any extension of shelf life beyond 24 months will be supported by real-time data.

11.3 In addition, the first three commercial-scale batches will be placed on stability under STAB-012, and at least one commercial batch per year thereafter will be placed in each marketed container closure system.
