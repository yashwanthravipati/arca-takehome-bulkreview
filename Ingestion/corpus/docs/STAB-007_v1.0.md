# STAB-007: Long-Term Stability Protocol for Calvidronate Concentrate for Solution for Infusion 5 mg/mL

Document number: STAB-007 | Version: 1.0 | Effective date: 2022-08-30 | Document type: Stability protocol | Product: Product C, Calvidronate concentrate for solution for infusion 5 mg/mL | Owner: Quality Control, Stability Unit, Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals, Harwick Manufacturing Site

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines the design, conduct and evaluation of the formal long-term and accelerated stability studies for Calvidronate concentrate for solution for infusion 5 mg/mL (Product C). The data generated under this protocol are intended to support the proposed shelf life and storage statement for the registration dossier, in accordance with ICH Q1A(R2), Q1D and Q1E. This protocol also documents the bracketing design applied to the three fill volumes and the stability commitment to be made at the time of marketing authorisation.

## 2. Scope

This protocol applies to registration stability batches of Calvidronate concentrate for solution for infusion 5 mg/mL filled into 4 mL, 10 mL and 20 mL Type I glass vials, manufactured at the Harwick Manufacturing Site and intended for refrigerated storage.

The following studies are outside the scope of this protocol and are governed by separate documents:

- In-use stability of the product after dilution in 0.9% sodium chloride for infusion, which is covered by STAB-008 (In-Use Stability Protocol for Calvidronate After Dilution in 0.9% Sodium Chloride).
- Annual ongoing stability of commercial batches, which is covered by STAB-009 (Annual Ongoing Stability Protocol for Commercial Calvidronate Concentrate).
- Photostability testing performed per ICH Q1B, which is conducted as a one-off confirmatory study on a single batch under SOP-QC-0318 and reported separately.
- Forced degradation studies supporting method validation, which are reported in the validation reports for the methods listed in Section 5.

## 4. Materials and batches

### 4.1 Product description

Calvidronate concentrate for solution for infusion is a clear, colourless, sterile aqueous solution containing 5 mg/mL calvidronate (as calvidronate sodium), buffered with sodium citrate and adjusted with sodium hydroxide and hydrochloric acid. The solution is manufactured as a single bulk formulation, sterile filtered and aseptically filled into vials.

### 4.2 Container closure system

All three fill volumes use the same container closure system components, differing only in vial nominal capacity:

## 5. Analytical methods

Testing is performed using the validated methods listed below. Methods are stability indicating where relevant, as demonstrated by forced degradation (acid, base, oxidative, thermal and photolytic stress). Any change to a method during the study must be managed through change control and supported by a bridging assessment approved by QA.

| Test | Method number | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance and colour of solution | AM-C-010 | Visual inspection against white and black backgrounds; Ph. Eur. 2.2.2 |
| Visible particles | AM-C-011 | Visual inspection, Ph. Eur. 2.9.20 |
| Assay (calvidronate) | AM-C-021 | Ion-pair reversed-phase HPLC with UV detection at 215 nm |
| Related substances (CVD-oxide, other specified and unspecified impurities, total impurities) | AM-C-022 | Gradient ion-pair reversed-phase HPLC with UV detection at 215 nm |
| pH | AM-C-030 | Potentiometry, Ph. Eur. 2.2.3 |
| Sub-visible particles | AM-C-031 | Light obscuration, Ph. Eur. 2.9.19, Method 1 |
| Extractable volume | AM-C-032 | Gravimetric, Ph. Eur. 2.9.17 |
| Bacterial endotoxins | AM-C-040 | Kinetic chromogenic LAL, Ph. Eur. 2.6.14 |
| Sterility | AM-C-041 | Membrane filtration, Ph. Eur. 2.6.1 |
| Container closure integrity | AM-C-045 | High-voltage leak detection, validated against laser-drilled positive controls |

CVD-oxide (the N-oxide degradant of calvidronate) is reported as area percent relative to calvidronate using a relative response factor of 1.08, as established in validation report VR-AM-C-022. The reporting threshold for related substances is 0.05%.

## 6. Storage conditions

The product is intended for refrigerated storage, and the study conditions have been selected according to ICH Q1A(R2) for drug products intended for storage in a refrigerator. Vials will be stored upright and inverted at 5°C ± 3°C for long-term testing and at 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH for accelerated testing. Inverted storage is included to assess the worst case of continuous contact between the solution and the stopper, which is relevant to leachables, sub-visible particle formation and potential sorption.

| Study | Condition | Chamber ID | Chamber location | Orientations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long-term | 5°C ± 3°C | SC-05-03 (backup SC-05-04) | Stability Suite B, Room B-112 | Upright and inverted |
| Accelerated | 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH | SC-25-07 (backup SC-25-08) | Stability Suite B, Room B-114 | Upright and inverted |

Chambers are qualified per SOP-ENG-0145 and continuously monitored by the site environmental monitoring system, with alarms at the limits stated above. An intermediate condition is not applied, as ICH Q1A(R2) does not require one for products intended for refrigerated storage; any significant change at the accelerated condition will be evaluated per Section 8.3. Temperature excursions are managed in accordance with SOP-QC-0309 (Stability Chamber Excursions) and Section 9 of this protocol.

## 7. Testing schedule

Long-term samples will be tested at 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18 and 24 months, and accelerated samples at 0, 3 and 6 months. The initial (time 0) results are the release results for each batch, supplemented by sub-visible particle and container closure integrity testing on both orientations performed within 14 days of placement. The pull window is ± 7 days for the 3-month timepoint and ± 14 days for all later timepoints. Testing must begin within 21 days of the pull date; pulled samples awaiting testing are held at 5°C ± 3°C.

### 7.1 Test matrix

| Test | Long-term 5°C ± 3°C | Accelerated 25°C/60% RH |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance and colour | All timepoints | All timepoints |
| Visible particles | All timepoints | All timepoints |
| Assay | All timepoints | All timepoints |
| Related substances | All timepoints | All timepoints |
| pH | All timepoints | All timepoints |
| Sub-visible particles | All timepoints | All timepoints |
| Extractable volume | 0, 12, 24 months | 0, 6 months |

All tests are performed on each batch in both orientations at each scheduled timepoint, except sterility and bacterial endotoxins, which are performed on inverted vials only as the worst case.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

### 8.1 Shelf-life acceptance criteria

The acceptance criteria applied in this protocol are the shelf-life criteria of SPEC-C. Release criteria in SPEC-C are tighter for certain parameters (for example, assay 97.0–103.0% of label claim and CVD-oxide not more than 0.2% at release) and are not applied to stability timepoints. Samples must comply with assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, CVD-oxide not more than 0.5%, total impurities not more than 2.0%, pH 6.8 to 7.6, and sub-visible particle limits of not more than 6000 per container at 10 µm or greater and not more than 600 per container at 25 µm or greater.

| Test | Shelf-life acceptance criterion |
|---|---|
| Appearance and colour | Clear, colourless solution; not more intensely coloured than reference solution Y7 |
| Visible particles | Practically free from visible particles |
| Assay | 95.0–105.0% of label claim |
| CVD-oxide | NMT 0.5% |
| Any unspecified impurity | NMT 0.2% |
| Total impurities | NMT 2.0% |
| pH | 6.8–7.6 |
| Sub-visible particles ≥ 10 µm | NMT 6000 per container |
| Sub-visible particles ≥ 25 µm | NMT 600 per container |
| Extractable volume | Not less than nominal volume |
| Bacterial endotoxins | NMT 1.4 EU/mg calvidronate |
| Sterility | Sterile |
| Container closure integrity | No leaking containers detected |

### 8.2 Trending and out-of-trend evaluation

Results for assay, CVD-oxide, total impurities and pH are trended at each timepoint by batch and orientation. An out-of-trend (OOT) result is defined per SOP-QC-0311 using a regression-based prediction interval. OOT results are investigated but do not in themselves constitute a failure.

### 8.3 Significant change and data evaluation

Significant change at the accelerated condition is defined according to ICH Q1A(R2) and includes a 5% change in assay from the initial value, any degradation product exceeding its acceptance criterion, failure of pH, or failure of appearance. Where significant change occurs at the accelerated condition, the proposed shelf life will be based on long-term data, and Regulatory Affairs will assess whether a label statement regarding excursions is supportable. Shelf life will be estimated in accordance with ICH Q1E, using regression analysis of the long-term data with one-sided 95% confidence limits for assay and degradants, and with poolability of batches tested at the 25% significance level. Extrapolation beyond the available long-term data will be limited as permitted by ICH Q1E for refrigerated products.

## 9. Deviation handling

Any departure from this protocol, including missed or late pulls, chamber excursions outside the stated limits, sample mix-ups, method deviations, and out-of-specification (OOS) results, must be recorded and managed as follows:

1. The person identifying the event notifies the Stability Coordinator and QC Analytical Manager on the same working day.
2. The event is recorded in the site quality management system per SOP-QA-0201 (Deviation Management) within one working day, with an initial impact assessment.
3. Chamber excursions are assessed per SOP-QC-0309, taking into account excursion duration, magnitude, and available supportive data. Affected samples may be transferred to the backup chamber identified in Section 6.
4. OOS results are investigated per SOP-QC-0310 (Out-of-Specification Investigations). Retesting from reserve samples is only permitted where authorised within the investigation.
5. QA approves the deviation closure, including any corrective and preventive actions and the impact on the validity of the stability data.
6. All deviations are summarised in the interim and final stability reports.

No deviations have been recorded under this protocol at the time of issue.

## 10. Bracketing and matrixing

Bracketing on fill volume is applied: the 4 mL and 20 mL presentations are tested, and the 10 mL presentation is covered because all three use the same Type I glass, stopper and solution. The design follows ICH Q1D. The 4 mL and 20 mL presentations represent the extremes of fill volume and of headspace-to-fill ratio and surface-area-to-volume ratio, so the stability of the intermediate 10 mL presentation is expected to lie within that of the extremes. The vials are produced by the same supplier from the same glass type, the stopper and seal are identical in formulation and dimensions, and the solution is manufactured from a single bulk formulation by the same process.

If the results for the two extremes differ such that the 10 mL presentation cannot be assumed to be bracketed, the 10 mL presentation will be considered as stable as the least stable extreme, and Regulatory Affairs will assess whether supplementary data are needed.

No matrixing is applied. All batches, both tested fill volumes and both orientations are tested at every scheduled timepoint for the tests specified in Section 7.1.

## 11. Post-approval commitments

The registration data will include the long-term data available at the time of submission, with the remaining timepoints reported when completed. The applicant commits to placing the first three commercial batches of each fill volume on long-term stability at 5°C ± 3°C through the approved shelf life. This commitment includes the 10 mL presentation, which is not tested in this registration study because of the bracketing design described in Section 10. Commitment batches will be tested according to the test schedule and acceptance criteria defined in this protocol, or in an approved successor protocol. After completing the commitment, ongoing annual stability monitoring of commercial batches will continue under STAB-009. Regulatory Affairs is responsible for tracking the commitment and for notifying the competent authorities of any confirmed OOS results in commitment batches.
