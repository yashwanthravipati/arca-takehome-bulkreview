# Ongoing Stability Program with Matrixing Design for Zentoprazine Extended-Release Tablets

**Document number:** STAB-015
**Version:** 1.1
**Effective date:** 2024-12-09
**Status:** Current
**Document type:** Stability protocol
**Product:** Product E, Zentoprazine Extended-Release Tablets, 50 mg, 100 mg and 200 mg, in HDPE bottles
**Owner:** Stability and Analytical Sciences, Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals, Harwell Road Quality Operations

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This protocol defines the ongoing (commercial) stability program for Zentoprazine Extended-Release Tablets 50 mg, 100 mg and 200 mg packaged in high-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles. The program confirms that commercial batches continue to meet the approved specification throughout the labelled shelf life of 36 months. It also provides the data needed to detect adverse trends that may arise from changes in raw materials, equipment or process drift.

1.2 The program uses a matrixing design in accordance with ICH Q1D. The design reduces the number of intermediate timepoints tested without reducing the program's ability to support the shelf life of each strength.

1.3 This protocol fulfils the post-approval stability commitment made in the marketing application. It operates alongside the registration data generated under STAB-013 and the site-transfer data generated under STAB-014.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This protocol applies to commercial batches of Zentoprazine Extended-Release Tablets in all three strengths. It covers batches manufactured at the Aldencourt Harwell Road facility and, from the date of regulatory approval of the second site, batches manufactured at the Lindgren site.

2.2 The commercial presentation covered is the 30-count HDPE bottle. This is a 60 cc white HDPE bottle with a 33 mm child-resistant polypropylene closure, an induction-sealed aluminium liner and a 1 g silica gel desiccant canister. The 90-count presentation uses the same bottle resin, closure and desiccant. It is represented by the 30-count bottle, which has the higher headspace-to-tablet ratio and is therefore the worst case. This approach is supported by the registration data in STAB-013.

2.3 This protocol does not cover the following, which are governed by STAB-013 and STAB-014 respectively:

- accelerated (40°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH) studies;
- intermediate (30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH) studies;
- photostability studies;
- registration batches;
- batches placed on stability to support a site change.

Batches placed on stability under this protocol because of a deviation or change control are added as supplementary batches in accordance with Section 9.

## 3. Responsibilities

3.1 **Stability Coordinator (Stability and Analytical Sciences):**

- schedules enrollment of batches;
- issues pull lists in the stability management system (LIMS module STB);
- maintains chamber inventory records;
- compiles the annual stability summary report.

3.2 **Quality Control Laboratory:**

- performs testing in accordance with the methods listed in Section 5;
- records results in LIMS;
- initiates out-of-specification (OOS) and out-of-trend (OOT) investigations in accordance with SOP-QC-0412 and SOP-QC-0418.

3.3 **Facilities and Engineering:**

- maintains, calibrates and qualifies the stability chambers;
- responds to chamber alarms in accordance with SOP-ENG-0230.

3.4 **Quality Assurance:**

- approves this protocol and any amendments;
- reviews and approves deviations;
- approves the annual stability summary report;
- confirms that results are reflected in the Annual Product Quality Review.

3.5 **Regulatory Affairs:**

- confirms that the program meets the post-approval commitment;
- assesses whether any finding requires notification to health authorities;
- maintains alignment between this protocol and the registered stability sections of the dossier.

The batch is selected by the Stability Coordinator from the first three packaged batches of that strength released in the year. Batches that have been reprocessed are excluded from routine enrollment. Batches that are subject to an open deviation affecting product quality are also excluded. Either type may be placed as supplementary batches by QA decision.

4.2 Batch numbers follow the Aldencourt convention ZT, then a two-digit manufacturing year, then a strength code, then a sequence number. The strength codes are A for 50 mg, C for 100 mg and E for 200 mg.

## 5. Analytical Methods

5.1 All testing is performed using the validated methods listed in Table 4. Each method is validated in accordance with ICH Q2(R2) and is stability-indicating where applicable. The current effective version of each method applies at the time of testing. A change of method version within a batch's study requires a QC-approved bridging assessment in accordance with SOP-QC-0455.

**Table 4. Analytical methods**

| Test | Method | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | AM-GEN-010 | Visual examination |
| Assay | AM-ZTP-001 | Reversed-phase HPLC, UV 254 nm |
| Related substances (ZTP N-oxide, ZTP desmethyl, unspecified, total) | AM-ZTP-002 | Gradient reversed-phase HPLC, UV 230 nm |
| Drug release | AM-ZTP-004 | USP <711> Apparatus 2, 50 rpm, 900 mL phosphate buffer pH 6.8, 37.0°C ± 0.5°C, HPLC finish |
| Water content | AM-GEN-022 | Coulometric Karl Fischer |
| Microbial enumeration | AM-MIC-005 | USP <61>/<62> |

5.2 The drug release method samples at 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 and 16 hours. Only the 2, 8 and 16 hour points carry acceptance criteria. The other points are recorded for profile comparison and trending.

## 6. Storage Conditions

6.1 Ongoing batches are stored at 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH. The long-term condition corresponds to ICH climatic zones I and II and to the labelled storage statement "Store at 20°C to 25°C; excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C."

<!-- page 4 -->

6.2 The primary chamber is walk-in chamber SC-07 in the Harwell Road stability suite (Room 1.14). It is qualified in accordance with SOP-ENG-0215 and mapped annually. Chamber SC-12 is designated as the backup long-term chamber. SC-12 is qualified at the same set point and is used only during planned maintenance of SC-07 or following an alarm that cannot be resolved within the response time defined in SOP-ENG-0230.

6.3 Chamber SC-09 in the same room operates at 30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH. It holds intermediate-condition samples for STAB-014. Chamber SC-04 operates at 40°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH and holds accelerated samples for STAB-013. Neither chamber is used for samples under this protocol.

6.4 Temperature and relative humidity are recorded continuously by the validated monitoring system at 5-minute intervals. Excursions outside the set-point tolerances for more than 4 hours, or any excursion more than 5°C or 10% RH from set point, are handled as deviations in accordance with Section 9.

6.5 From version 1.1 onwards, sample transfers between chambers require a second-person verification of the destination chamber ID. The verification is recorded on form FRM-STB-031.

## 7. Testing Schedule

7.1 Each annual batch is tested at 0, 12, 24 and 36 months, with the 6-month and 18-month timepoints matrixed across alternate batches. Batches assigned to matrix group M6 are tested at 6 months and not at 18 months. Batches assigned to matrix group M18 are tested at 18 months and not at 6 months. Matrix group assignment alternates from year to year for each strength, as shown in Table 1. Over any two consecutive program years, each strength therefore contributes data at both matrixed timepoints.

7.2 The pull window is ±7 days for the 6-month timepoint and ±14 days for all later timepoints. Testing is completed within 30 days of the pull date. Pulled samples awaiting testing are held at 2–8°C in refrigerator RF-03 in the QC laboratory.

## 8. Acceptance Criteria

8.1 Results are compared with the current specification: assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, ZTP N-oxide not more than 0.3%, total impurities not more than 1.2%, and drug release of 15–35% at 2 hours, 50–75% at 8 hours and not less than 85% at 16 hours. These shelf-life limits are those of SPEC-E, which takes precedence if any difference arises. Table 6 lists the full set of shelf-life criteria applied under this protocol, including attributes not listed above.

**Table 6. Shelf-life acceptance criteria (per SPEC-E)**

| Test | Acceptance criterion |
|---|---|
| Appearance | Film-coated, capsule-shaped tablets with debossing per strength; no cracking, chipping or discolouration |
| Assay | 95.0–105.0% of label claim |
| ZTP N-oxide | NMT 0.3% |
| ZTP desmethyl | NMT 0.2% |
| Any unspecified impurity | NMT 0.10% |
| Total impurities | NMT 1.2% |
| Drug release, 2 h | 15–35% |
| Drug release, 8 h | 50–75% |
| Drug release, 16 h | NLT 85% |
| Water content | NMT 3.0% w/w |
| TAMC / TYMC | NMT 10³ CFU/g / NMT 10² CFU/g |
| *E. coli* | Absent in 1 g |

8.2 Drug release is evaluated against the USP <711> acceptance table for extended-release dosage forms, using levels L1 to L3.

8.3 Results within specification are also trended. An OOT result is defined in SOP-QC-0418 as either of the following:

- a result outside the 95% prediction interval of the regression for that batch;
- a change from the previous timepoint greater than 2.0% of label claim for assay, or greater than 0.10% for ZTP N-oxide.

## 9. Deviation Handling

9.1 Any departure from this protocol is recorded as a deviation in the Aldencourt quality management system in accordance with SOP-QA-0107. Examples include:

- a missed or late pull;
- a chamber excursion;
- sample mix-up;
- testing outside the time limit;
- use of an unapproved method version.

QA classifies each deviation as minor, major or critical. A root cause investigation is completed for all deviations classified as major or critical. Deviation numbers follow the Aldencourt fiscal-year register; fiscal year 2025 began on 1 October 2024.

9.2 Deviation DEV-2025-0089 recorded that 24-month samples of the 100 mg batch ZT22C017 were mistakenly placed in the 30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH chamber for 6 days, and the batch was retained in the program with a supplementary 27-month test. The error occurred during the November 2024 requalification of SC-07. The containers designated for the 24-month pull were moved to a temporary location, and on return they were loaded into SC-09 rather than SC-12. The error was detected at the scheduled pull.

<!-- page 6 -->

9.3 The investigation for DEV-2025-0089 considered several factors. The excursion was short. The containers were induction-sealed and contained desiccant. Accelerated and intermediate data for Zentoprazine in STAB-013 and STAB-014 show only minor changes at 30°C / 65% RH over 12 months. On this basis, QA concluded that the exposure was unlikely to affect the 24-month results materially. The 24-month results were reported with a cross-reference to the deviation.

9.4 To confirm the conclusion in 9.3, three reserve containers that had remained at 25°C / 60% RH throughout are tested at a supplementary 27-month timepoint, scheduled for February 2025. They are tested against the full Table 6 criteria. The 27-month results will be compared with the 24-month results. If the comparison shows a meaningful difference, the 24-month data for ZT22C017 will be excluded from shelf-life regression. The corrective and preventive actions are the second-person verification described in 6.5 and the increase in reserve containers described in Table 2.

9.5 Confirmed OOS results on stability are reported to QA within one working day. Regulatory Affairs assesses the need for a field alert or other notification within three working days.

## 10. Bracketing and Matrixing

10.1 This program uses matrixing and does not use bracketing. All three strengths are enrolled and tested every year. Bracketing was considered and rejected for this protocol. The strengths are not direct compression-weight proportional at the release-controlling polymer level, and the 100 mg strength is commercially the highest-volume strength.

10.2 Matrixing is justified because registration data showed low variability across strengths, and the reduced design still tests every strength at the first and final timepoints. The registration study under STAB-013 included three batches of each strength tested at all timepoints. Statistical evaluation in accordance with ICH Q1E showed common slopes and intercepts across strengths for the following attributes: assay, ZTP N-oxide, total impurities, and 8-hour drug release. Analysis of covariance found no significant strength effect, with p greater than 0.25 for all four. The residual standard deviation for assay was 0.6% of label claim.

10.3 The design is a one-half reduction at the intermediate timepoints only. Full testing is maintained at 0, 12, 24 and 36 months. Full testing at 12 months provides a fixed anchor before the shelf-life-relevant timepoints. Full testing at 36 months confirms the labelled expiry for every batch.

10.4 Matrixing is suspended if any of the following occurs:

- a confirmed OOS result;
- two consecutive OOT results for the same attribute in any batch;
- a change control classified as major that affects formulation, process or container closure.

<!-- page 7 -->

While matrixing is suspended, all enrolled batches are tested at both 6 and 18 months until QA and Regulatory Affairs approve reinstatement.

## 11. Post-approval Commitments

11.1 The holder commits to enrolling one batch of each strength per year, rotating strengths under the matrixing design, for the life of the product. In any year in which a strength is not manufactured, the commitment is met by enrolling the first batch of that strength manufactured in the following year. In addition, the Annual Product Quality Review records why no batch was enrolled.

11.2 Data from this program are summarised annually in a stability report. The report includes regression analyses in accordance with ICH Q1E and is submitted in the annual report to the relevant health authorities.

11.3 Once the Lindgren site is approved, the first three commercial batches from that site remain under STAB-014 until that study is complete. Thereafter, Lindgren batches enter this program on the same annual basis, and enrollment is split between sites in proportion to annual production volume.
