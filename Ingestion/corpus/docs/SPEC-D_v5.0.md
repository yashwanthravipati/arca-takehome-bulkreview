## 1. Purpose

1.1 This specification defines the tests, analytical procedures and acceptance criteria that Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules (Product D) must meet at batch release and throughout the approved shelf life. It is the controlling document for batch disposition by Quality Assurance and for evaluating stability results generated under the stability protocols listed in Section 8.

1.2 This specification also records the rationale for each acceptance criterion and the analytical procedures, reference standards and supporting documents. Section 5 summarises the justification. The full justification is held in the regulatory dossier (Module 3.2.P.5.6).

## 2. Scope

2.1 This specification applies to all commercial batches of Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules manufactured at the Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals Harrowfield site (Building 2, Solid Oral Dose Suite C) and packaged in either of the following:

- PVC/PVDC (250 µm / 60 g/m²) blisters with 20 µm hard-temper aluminium lidding foil, 10 capsules per blister card; or
- white HDPE bottles with induction-sealed polypropylene child-resistant closures and a 1 g silica gel desiccant canister, in 30-, 60- and 90-count presentations.

2.2 The specification applies to batches filled into HPMC capsule shells from the primary approved supplier. It also applies to batches filled into shells from the alternate supplier, once the data generated under STAB-011 have been approved by Regulatory Affairs and the variation has been granted.

2.3 Release testing is performed on the bulk-filled capsules or on the packaged product, as indicated in Table 2. Shelf-life criteria apply to samples taken under STAB-010 (registration stability) and STAB-012 (ongoing stability), and to any market complaint or retained-sample investigation.

2.4 This specification does not cover the moxilurine drug substance, which is controlled under its own drug substance specification. It also does not cover in-process controls, which are defined in the master batch record MBR-D-150.

## 3. Product description and composition

3.1 Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules are size 1 opaque capsules with a white body and a dark blue cap, printed "MXL 150" in black ink on the body. Each capsule contains a white to off-white free-flowing powder blend. The capsule shells are hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC) gelled with gellan gum. They contain no material of animal origin.

4.2 Footnotes to Table 2:

a) Degradation products are reported as % area relative to moxilurine, with relative response factors applied as given in ATM-D-120 (MXL-dimer RRF 0.87; MXL-N-oxide RRF 1.04). Results at or above 0.05% (reporting threshold) are reported to two decimal places. Results below 0.05% are reported as "< 0.05%" and are not included in the total.

b) Dissolution is tested to Ph. Eur. 2.9.3 / USP <711> acceptance tables (S1, S2, S3).

c) Microbiological testing is performed at release on every batch. On stability, it is performed at the initial, 12-month, 24-month and 36-month time points only, as specified in STAB-012.

d) Process-related impurities of the drug substance, MXL-chloro intermediate and MXL-desmethyl, are controlled in the drug substance specification. Neither increases on storage, and they are not monitored in the drug product (see Section 5.4).

4.3 The four attributes most relevant to clinical performance and product safety have identical release and shelf-life limits, so the same limit applies at every stage of the product life cycle. The current acceptance criteria for Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules are assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, MXL-dimer not more than 0.2%, total impurities not more than 1.0%, and dissolution not less than 75% (Q) in 45 minutes. Release limits are tighter than shelf-life limits only for MXL-N-oxide and water content. The rationale is given in Section 5.

## 5. Justification of specification

5.1 **General.** The specification was established in accordance with ICH Q6A. It is based on the following data:

- batch analysis data from three registration batches (D24001, D24002 and D24003, each 180,000 capsules) and six commercial batches (D24011 to D24016);
- registration stability data generated under STAB-010;
- forced degradation studies reported in development report DR-D-021.

5.2 **Assay.** The release and shelf-life limits of 95.0–105.0% of label claim are standard for an immediate-release oral solid dosage form. Release assay results for the nine batches ranged from 98.4% to 101.2%. Under STAB-010, assay fell by no more than 1.1% after 36 months at 30 °C/75% RH (chamber SC-07) and by no more than 1.8% after 6 months at 40 °C/75% RH (chamber SC-09) in either presentation. The data show no need for a wider shelf-life limit.

5.3 **MXL-dimer.** MXL-dimer is the principal degradation product. It forms by a moisture-mediated condensation reaction. The maximum daily dose of moxilurine is 300 mg (two capsules). At this dose, the ICH Q3B(R2) identification and qualification thresholds are both 0.2%. The limit of NMT 0.2% therefore requires no additional qualification. At release, MXL-dimer ranged from < 0.05% to 0.06%. The highest stability result was 0.13% after 36 months at 30 °C/75% RH (batch D24002, PVC/PVDC blister). Version 4.0 applied a provisional limit of NMT 0.3%, supported by a toxicological qualification study. In version 5.0, the limit was tightened to NMT 0.2% at both release and shelf life, in line with the observed data and with the qualification threshold (change control CC-2025-0147).

<!-- page 4 -->

5.4 **MXL-N-oxide.** MXL-N-oxide is an oxidative degradant. It has been qualified to 0.5% in the 13-week rat toxicity study (report TOX-MXL-0093). The highest stability result was 0.17% (36 months, 30 °C/75% RH, 90-count HDPE bottle). The release limit of NMT 0.2% allows for the increase observed on storage, so that a batch which passes at release will meet the shelf-life limit of NMT 0.3% at expiry.

5.5 **Unspecified degradation products and total.** The limit for any unspecified degradation product is set at NMT 0.10%. This is below the identification threshold and is supported by the absence of any unspecified peak above 0.07% in any batch or stability sample. The total limit of NMT 1.0% includes the specified and unspecified degradation products. The highest total observed was 0.41%.

5.6 **Dissolution.** The dissolution method was selected for its discriminating power. During development (DR-D-018), it distinguished batches with ±20% croscarmellose sodium and batches blended at the high and low limits of lubrication time. Mean dissolution at 45 minutes ranged from 89% to 96% at release and from 86% to 94% at 36 months.

The acceptance criterion of Q = 75% at 45 minutes replaced the version 3.0 criterion of Q = 80% at 30 minutes. The change was made because HPMC shells open more slowly than gelatin shells in phosphate buffer. The approved bioequivalence batch D23B07 released 84% (mean) at 45 minutes, and the criterion was set relative to this batch. The criterion is also being confirmed for alternate-supplier shells under STAB-011.

5.7 **Water content.** HPMC shells hold less water than gelatin shells and are less brittle at low humidity. The capsule-plus-contents water content was 4.1–5.2% at release. In blisters at 30 °C/75% RH, it rose to a maximum of 6.4%. The shelf-life limit of NMT 7.0% is set below the level at which MXL-dimer formation accelerated in the humidity-stress study (DR-D-021, section 6.3). The release limit of NMT 6.0% provides a margin for the increase observed on storage.

5.8 **Tests not included.** The following tests are not included in the specification:

- Disintegration is not included because dissolution is tested.
- Chiral purity is not included because moxilurine has no stereocentre.
- Polymorph testing in the drug product is not included because the drug substance (Form I) showed no conversion in the drug product under any stability condition (XRPD, DR-D-024).

## 6. Analytical procedures

6.1 All analytical procedures are validated in accordance with ICH Q2(R2). Validation reports are listed in Table 3. Testing is performed by Quality Control, Harrowfield site (Building 4, Laboratories 4.12 and 4.14), under SOP-QC-0201 (Execution of Analytical Test Methods). Out-of-specification results are handled under SOP-QA-0450.

<!-- page 5 -->

**Table 3. Analytical procedures**

| Method | Title | Technique | Validation report |
|---|---|---|---|
| ATM-D-001 | Appearance of Moxilurine Capsules | Visual | Not applicable |
| ATM-D-110 | Identification, Assay and Content Uniformity of Moxilurine Capsules | RP-HPLC with DAD | VR-D-110 v2 |
| ATM-D-120 | Degradation Products in Moxilurine Capsules | RP-HPLC gradient, UV 254 nm | VR-D-120 v3 |
| ATM-D-130 | Dissolution of Moxilurine 150 mg Capsules | Ph. Eur. 2.9.3 apparatus 2, HPLC finish | VR-D-130 v2 |
| ATM-D-140 | Water Content of Moxilurine Capsules | Coulometric Karl Fischer with oven at 140 °C | VR-D-140 v1 |
| ATM-D-150 | Microbial Enumeration, Moxilurine Capsules | Ph. Eur. 2.6.12, pour plate | VR-D-150 v1 |
| ATM-D-151 | Test for Specified Microorganisms, Moxilurine Capsules | Ph. Eur. 2.6.13 | VR-D-151 v1 |

6.2 **ATM-D-110 (assay, identification and content uniformity).** The method uses the following conditions:

- Column: C18, 150 × 4.6 mm, 3.5 µm, at 35 °C.
- Mobile phase: isocratic, 0.02 M potassium phosphate pH 3.0 / acetonitrile (60:40).
- Flow rate: 1.0 mL/min.
- Detection: 254 nm.

For assay, a composite of 20 capsules is prepared at a nominal concentration of 0.15 mg/mL. For content uniformity, 10 individual capsules are tested.

System suitability requirements are:

- RSD of five replicate standard injections NMT 1.0%;
- tailing factor NMT 1.5;
- standard check agreement 98.0–102.0%.

6.3 **ATM-D-120 (degradation products).** The method uses the same column type as ATM-D-110, with a 35-minute gradient from 15% to 70% acetonitrile. The sample concentration is 1.0 mg/mL.

System suitability requirements are:

- resolution between MXL-N-oxide and moxilurine NLT 2.0;
- resolution between moxilurine and MXL-dimer NLT 3.0;
- signal-to-noise ratio for the 0.05% sensitivity solution NLT 10.

The limit of quantitation is 0.03% for both specified degradation products.

6.4 **ATM-D-130 (dissolution).** The method uses the following conditions:

- Apparatus: Ph. Eur. 2.9.3 apparatus 2 (paddle) at 50 rpm, with three-prong wire sinkers.
- Medium: 900 mL of 0.05 M phosphate buffer pH 6.8, at 37.0 ± 0.5 °C.
- Sampling: 10 mL at 45 minutes, filtered through a 10 µm full-flow filter.
- Quantitation: by HPLC against a reference standard solution of 0.167 mg/mL.

For product development and investigations only, a profile at 10, 15, 20, 30, 45 and 60 minutes is available as an option.

6.5 **Sample quantities.** The quantities required for a full release test are:

<!-- page 6 -->

- 20 capsules for assay;
- 10 capsules for uniformity;
- 10 capsules for degradation products;
- 24 capsules for dissolution (S1 to S3);
- 10 capsules for water;
- 30 capsules for microbiology.

The total is 104 capsules per batch. The QC sampling plan SP-D-150 adds 40 capsules for retest reserve and 120 capsules for retention.
