# STAB-008 In-Use Stability Protocol for Calvidronate After Dilution in 0.9% Sodium Chloride

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This protocol defines the design, conduct and evaluation of an in-use stability study for Calvidronate concentrate for solution for infusion 5 mg/mL after dilution in 0.9% sodium chloride injection. The study generates the data needed to support the in-use storage statement in the prescribing information. It covers the period between preparation of the infusion and the end of administration.

1.2 The study shows whether the diluted product keeps acceptable quality under the storage conditions likely to occur in clinical practice. These conditions are refrigerated holding in the pharmacy and ward, and holding at controlled room temperature under normal room lighting during the preparation-to-infusion interval.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This protocol applies to Calvidronate concentrate 5 mg/mL diluted to clinical concentrations in 0.9% sodium chloride injection in polyolefin infusion bags. It covers the preparation of the diluted solutions, their storage, sampling, testing and data evaluation.

2.2 This protocol does not cover the stability of the undiluted concentrate in its primary container. That is governed by STAB-007 (Long-Term Stability Protocol for Calvidronate Concentrate for Solution for Infusion 5 mg/mL) for registration batches and by STAB-009 (Annual Ongoing Stability Protocol for Commercial Calvidronate Concentrate) for commercial batches. The long-term storage condition for the concentrate (5°C ± 3°C) and the accelerated condition (25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH) defined in STAB-007 do not apply to diluted solutions under this protocol.

2.3 This protocol does not cover compatibility with infusion diluents other than 0.9% sodium chloride, such as 5% glucose. It also does not cover infusion containers other than polyolefin bags, or administration sets and in-line filters.

2.4 The study is performed at the Aldencourt Harrowfield Site, Quality Control Laboratory, Building 3.

## 3. Responsibilities

3.1 **Stability Group Lead (Pharmaceutical Development)** owns this protocol. The Lead coordinates sample preparation and chamber placement, keeps the stability schedule, and authors the in-use stability report.

3.2 **Quality Control Analytical Laboratory** prepares the diluted solutions as described in Section 4. It also pulls and tests samples using the methods in Section 5 and records results in the laboratory information management system (LIMS, study code IU-C-2403).

3.3 **Facilities and Engineering** qualifies, monitors and alarms the storage chambers and the room-temperature storage area. This includes continuous temperature logging and illuminance verification.

3.4 **Quality Assurance** approves this protocol and reviews any deviations or out-of-specification (OOS) results. QA also approves the final report before data are used to support labelling.

3.5 **Regulatory Affairs** checks that the protocol design and the final data package can support the in-use statement in the prescribing information.

## 4. Materials and Batches

4.1 Two batches of Calvidronate concentrate 5 mg/mL are used. One batch was recently manufactured. The second batch is from the registration stability programme and has been stored under STAB-007 long-term conditions, so it represents product late in its shelf life.

| Batch | Manufacture date | Vial size used | Storage before study | Release assay (% label claim) | Release CVD-oxide (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-23-0412 | 2023-11-14 | 4 mL and 20 mL | 2°C to 8°C, warehouse cold room CR-02 | 100.4 | 0.08 |
| C-22-0187 | 2022-06-21 | 4 mL and 20 mL | 2°C to 8°C, STAB-007 chamber CH-R-02 | 99.7 | 0.11 |

4.2 Before use, each batch must have been released against SPEC-C (Drug Product Specification: Calvidronate Concentrate for Solution for Infusion 5 mg/mL). The most recent STAB-007 time point for batch C-22-0187 must remain within SPEC-C shelf-life limits.

4.3 **Diluent and containers.** The diluent is 0.9% sodium chloride injection in 100 mL polyolefin infusion bags (Veraflex PO, lot VF-23K118; material code MAT-4471). Bags from a single lot are used throughout the study.

4.4 **Preparation of diluted solutions.** Solutions are prepared aseptically in a Grade A laminar airflow cabinet (LAF-3.07) following SOP-QC-118, Preparation of Diluted Infusion Samples for In-Use Studies. The preparations are made as follows:

- **0.1 mg/mL:** Withdraw 2.0 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride from a 100 mL bag. Then add 2.0 mL of concentrate (10 mg calvidronate) from a 4 mL vial.
- **1.0 mg/mL:** Withdraw 20.0 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride from a 100 mL bag. Then add 20.0 mL of concentrate (100 mg calvidronate) from a 20 mL vial.

Each bag is inverted ten times to mix and labelled with study code, batch, concentration, condition, replicate and time of preparation. The time of preparation is defined as the time the concentrate addition is complete. It is recorded to the nearest minute and is the reference for all time points.

## 5. Analytical Methods

5.1 All testing uses validated methods. Each method has been shown to be suitable for the diluted matrix at both 0.1 mg/mL and 1.0 mg/mL. Suitability was established in report VR-C-2311, Matrix Suitability of Calvidronate Methods in 0.9% Sodium Chloride.

| Test | Method number | Principle | Sample volume per pull |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appearance (visible particulate matter, colour, clarity) | AM-C-018 | Visual inspection against black and white backgrounds, Ph. Eur. 2.9.20 | Intact bag and 10 mL aliquot in clear glass tube |
| Assay of calvidronate | AM-C-012 | Reversed-phase HPLC with UV detection at 215 nm | 3.0 mL |
| CVD-oxide and other related substances | AM-C-012 | Same chromatographic run as assay; CVD-oxide relative retention time 0.84 | (shared with assay) |
| pH | AM-C-015 | Potentiometry, Ph. Eur. 2.2.3 | 5.0 mL |
| Subvisible particles (information only) | AM-C-020 | Light obscuration, Ph. Eur. 2.9.19 | 25 mL (0 h and 48 h only) |
| Osmolality (information only) | AM-C-022 | Freezing-point depression | 0.5 mL (0 h and 48 h only) |

5.2 For the 1.0 mg/mL solutions, samples for AM-C-012 are diluted 1 in 10 with mobile phase A so that they fall within the validated range. The 0.1 mg/mL solutions are injected undiluted. Unspecified related substances are reported at or above the reporting threshold of 0.05%.

5.3 Aliquots are withdrawn through the bag injection port with a sterile syringe in the LAF cabinet. Analysis is completed within 2 hours of withdrawal. If analysis cannot start within this time, the aliquots are held at 2°C to 8°C in the dark for no longer than 4 hours.

## 6. Storage Conditions

6.1 Diluted infusion solutions will be held in polyolefin infusion bags at 2°C to 8°C and at 25°C ± 2°C under normal room lighting. These conditions represent refrigerated holding before administration and the period in which a prepared infusion stands on the ward or runs at room temperature.

<!-- page 4 -->

6.2 **Refrigerated condition.** Bags are stored flat on perforated shelving in stability chamber CH-R-04 (qualified range 2°C to 8°C, set point 5°C). The chamber has no internal lighting other than during door openings. Temperature is logged every 5 minutes by the site monitoring system (probe TM-R04-1). An alarm is raised on any excursion outside 2°C to 8°C lasting longer than 15 minutes.

6.3 **Room-temperature condition.** Bags are stored on an open bench in temperature-controlled laboratory room L3-214, which is qualified to 25°C ± 2°C. They are exposed to the room's overhead fluorescent lighting, which is kept on for the full study duration. Illuminance at bag level is measured at the start of the study and at each time point with calibrated lux meter LX-09. The illuminance is expected to be between 500 and 1,000 lux, representative of clinical areas. Temperature is logged every 5 minutes by data logger TL-214-3. Bags are placed so that no bag shades another.

6.4 This study is not a confirmatory photostability study under ICH Q1B. The photostability of the concentrate and drug substance was characterised during development and showed no significant change in the protected vial. The room-lighting condition here reflects use conditions and does not represent a forced light exposure.

## 7. Testing Schedule

7.1 Diluted samples will be analysed at 0, 6, 12, 24 and 48 hours after preparation. The 0-hour sample is taken from each bag within 15 minutes of preparation and before the bag is moved to its storage condition. Results at 0 hours establish the initial concentration for each bag. All later assay results are expressed against this value.

7.2 The 48-hour time point covers the longest in-use period that could reasonably be proposed in the prescribing information. The earlier time points show the shape of any change over time, so that a shorter in-use period can be justified if needed.

7.3 **Pull windows.** Each sample must be pulled within the following windows: ±10 minutes at 6 hours, ±20 minutes at 12 hours, ±30 minutes at 24 hours and ±1 hour at 48 hours. A pull outside its window is handled as a deviation according to Section 9.

| 12 | ±20 min | X | X | X | X | – | – |

7.4 The total volume withdrawn from each bag over the study is about 113 mL for the 0.1 mg/mL bags and 113 mL for the 1.0 mg/mL bags. This includes a 1 mL syringe flush at each pull. Visual appearance is assessed on the intact bag before any withdrawal at each time point. Because the withdrawals exceed the nominal 100 mL, the subvisible-particle aliquot at 48 hours is taken from the spare bag set where needed. When this happens, it is noted in the analytical record.

## 8. Acceptance Criteria

8.1 The diluted solution must retain assay of 95.0–105.0% of the initial concentration, CVD-oxide not more than 0.5%, and pH 6.8 to 7.6, with no visible particulate matter. These criteria apply to each individual bag at each time point under both storage conditions.

8.2 The criteria are summarised below together with the corresponding SPEC-C shelf-life limits for the undiluted concentrate, for comparison. The SPEC-C limits are given for context only and are not applied to the diluted solution.

| Attribute | In-use criterion (diluted solution, STAB-008) | SPEC-C shelf-life limit (concentrate, for reference) |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | No visible particulate matter | Clear, colourless solution, practically free from visible particles |
| Assay | 95.0–105.0% of initial (0 h) concentration | 95.0–105.0% of label claim |
| CVD-oxide | NMT 0.5% | NMT 0.5% |
| Unspecified related substances | Report | NMT 0.20% each |
| pH | 6.8–7.6 | 6.8–7.6 |
| Subvisible particles | Report (information only) | Ph. Eur. 2.9.19 limits |
| Osmolality | Report (information only) | Not applicable |

8.3 **Evaluation.** Results are evaluated by batch, concentration and condition. Trends in assay and CVD-oxide are assessed by visual inspection of plotted data and, where meaningful, by linear regression following the principles of ICH Q1E. Statistical extrapolation beyond 48 hours is not performed. The in-use period proposed for labelling is the longest time point at which all replicate bags meet every criterion for both batches and both concentrations. A separate period is proposed for each storage condition.

8.4 If the 1.0 mg/mL and 0.1 mg/mL concentrations behave differently, the more conservative result determines the proposed in-use period. Any result outside an acceptance criterion is investigated as described in Section 9.

## 9. Deviation Handling

9.1 Departures from this protocol are recorded and managed under SOP-QA-031, Deviation Management. Examples include pull-window exceedances, chamber or room temperature excursions, illuminance outside the expected range, preparation errors and sample loss. Each deviation receives a deviation number. It is assessed for impact on data integrity and on the study conclusions. QA must approve the deviation before affected data are reported.

<!-- page 6 -->

9.2 Results outside the acceptance criteria in Section 8 are investigated under SOP-QC-044, Investigation of Out-of-Specification and Out-of-Trend Results. For a result that is out of trend but within limits, the Stability Group Lead documents an evaluation in the study file.

9.3 Temperature excursions shorter than 15 minutes during door openings at CH-R-04 are expected and are not treated as deviations. However, they are captured in the chamber log. Any other excursion requires the affected bags to be identified and assessed under SOP-QA-031.

9.4 No deviations have been recorded under this protocol.

## 10. Bracketing and Matrixing

10.1 The prescribing information allows Calvidronate concentrate to be diluted to final concentrations between 0.1 mg/mL and 1.0 mg/mL in 0.9% sodium chloride. The lowest (0.1 mg/mL) and highest (1.0 mg/mL) clinical dilutions are studied so that all intermediate dilutions in the prescribing information are bracketed. This design is consistent with the bracketing principles of ICH Q1D. Its justification rests on the following points:

- **Same formulation and diluent.** All intermediate dilutions use the same formulation, diluent and container material. They differ only in the ratio of concentrate to saline.
- **Expected degradation route.** The main degradation pathway, oxidation to CVD-oxide, is expected to depend on concentration. The lowest concentration is the most sensitive to relative loss and to adsorption to the container. The highest concentration is the most sensitive to pH shift arising from the concentrate's buffer contribution.
- **Container material.** The polyolefin bag material is the same at every concentration, so any container interaction is captured at the extremes.

10.2 Different vial sizes (4 mL, 10 mL, 20 mL) contain the same formulation at the same concentration and are filled from the same bulk solution. After dilution, vial size has no effect on the composition of the infusion. The 4 mL and 20 mL vials are used because they are the most practical way to prepare the two bracketing concentrations. The 10 mL vial is not tested separately.

10.3 Matrixing is not applied. Every bag is tested at every scheduled time point for all attributes listed in Section 7, except the information-only tests restricted to 0 and 48 hours.
