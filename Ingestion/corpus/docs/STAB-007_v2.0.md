# Long-Term Stability Protocol for Calvidronate Concentrate for Solution for Infusion 5 mg/mL

**Document number:** STAB-007
**Version:** 2.0
**Effective date:** 2024-10-07
**Status:** Current
**Document type:** Stability protocol
**Site:** Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals, Kestrel Park Manufacturing and Quality Control Site, Building 3

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This protocol defines the design, storage conditions, testing schedule, analytical methods and acceptance criteria for the formal long-term and accelerated stability study of Calvidronate Concentrate for Solution for Infusion 5 mg/mL (Product C). The study supports the proposed shelf life and storage statement ("Store in a refrigerator (2°C–8°C). Do not freeze.") for the marketing authorisation application.

1.2 The data generated under this protocol will be evaluated in accordance with ICH Q1A(R2) and ICH Q1E. The data will be used to establish a shelf life of up to 36 months at the long-term condition and to assess the effect of short-term excursions outside the labelled storage condition.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This protocol applies to the registration stability batches of Calvidronate Concentrate for Solution for Infusion 5 mg/mL filled into 4 mL, 10 mL and 20 mL Type I clear borosilicate glass vials. Each vial is closed with a 20 mm grey chlorobutyl rubber stopper (fluoropolymer-coated) and an aluminium flip-off seal.

2.2 The following are outside the scope of this protocol:

- In-use stability after dilution in 0.9% sodium chloride, which is covered by STAB-008.
- Annual ongoing stability of commercial batches after approval, which is covered by STAB-009.
- Photostability testing to ICH Q1B. This testing was performed on one batch of each tested fill volume under study report SR-CVD-014. The results are referenced here only to support the storage statement.
- Freeze–thaw and temperature cycling studies, which are reported in SR-CVD-017.

2.3 The release and shelf-life specification for the drug product is SPEC-C. Where this protocol and SPEC-C differ, SPEC-C governs commercial release. This protocol governs the evaluation of registration stability data.

5.2 Inverted vials are tested for assay, related substances, pH and sub-visible particles in order to detect any interaction between the solution and the stopper. Sterility, endotoxins and container closure integrity are tested on upright vials only. The CCI method qualification under report VR-CVD-009 demonstrated that orientation does not affect the result.

## 6. Storage conditions

6.1 Storage conditions are selected in accordance with ICH Q1A(R2) for a drug product intended for storage in a refrigerator. Vials will be stored upright and inverted at 5°C ± 3°C for long-term testing and at 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH for accelerated testing. The inverted orientation places the solution in continuous contact with the stopper and represents the worst case for leachables and for sorption to the stopper.

6.2 Chamber allocation:

| Condition | Primary chamber | Back-up chamber | Mapping report |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5°C ± 3°C | SC-05-01 (walk-in, Room 3.14) | SC-05-02 (Room 3.14) | MAP-ENG-2023-031 |
| 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH | SC-25-03 (reach-in, Room 3.16) | SC-25-04 (Room 3.16) | MAP-ENG-2023-034 |

6.3 Chambers are monitored continuously by the validated environmental monitoring system (EMS-KP-01) with readings recorded at 5-minute intervals. Alarms are set at the limits of each condition, with a 15-minute delay. Any excursion outside the stated limits is assessed under SOP-ENG-0412. The assessment determines whether an excursion is a deviation and whether it affects the study.

6.4 A 30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH intermediate condition is not included. The accelerated condition already represents a significant change relative to the refrigerated long-term condition, as described in ICH Q1A(R2) Section 2.2.7.3. No samples are stored at the frozen condition, because the product is labelled "Do not freeze".

## 7. Testing schedule

7.1 Long-term samples will be tested at 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24 and 36 months, and accelerated samples at 0, 3 and 6 months. The initial (0-month) results are generated from release testing of each batch and are common to both conditions.

7.2 Samples are pulled within ± 7 days of the nominal pull date up to and including 12 months, and within ± 14 days thereafter. Testing must start within 30 days of the pull. During this period, pulled samples are stored at the study condition or at 5°C ± 3°C.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

8.1 The shelf-life acceptance criteria applied to this study are those defined in SPEC-C. The main stability-indicating attributes are summarised here. Samples must comply with assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, CVD-oxide not more than 0.5%, total impurities not more than 2.0%, pH 6.8 to 7.6, and sub-visible particle limits of not more than 6000 per container at 10 µm or greater and not more than 600 per container at 25 µm or greater.

<!-- page 5 -->

8.2 The following table lists all shelf-life criteria applied in this study. The tighter release limits in SPEC-C (for example, assay 97.0–103.0% of label claim) apply to batch release only and are not applied to stability samples.

| Attribute | Shelf-life acceptance criterion |
|---|---|
| Appearance | Clear, colourless to pale yellow solution, practically free from visible particles; container intact |
| Assay | 95.0–105.0% of label claim |
| CVD-oxide | NMT 0.5% |
| CVD-dimer | NMT 0.3% |
| Any unspecified impurity | NMT 0.20% |
| Total impurities | NMT 2.0% |
| pH | 6.8–7.6 |
| Sub-visible particles, ≥10 µm | NMT 6000 per container |
| Sub-visible particles, ≥25 µm | NMT 600 per container |
| Osmolality | 270–330 mOsm/kg |
| Extractable volume | Not less than nominal volume |
| Bacterial endotoxins | NMT 2.5 EU/mg calvidronate |
| Sterility | Sterile |
| Container closure integrity | No leaking container detected |

8.3 The diluted infusion solution is subject to different limits, which are defined in STAB-008. Those limits do not apply to this protocol.

8.4 A significant change at the accelerated condition is defined as in ICH Q1A(R2) Section 2.2.7.1. Specifically, it means one of the following:

- a 5% change in assay from the initial value;
- any degradation product exceeding its acceptance criterion;
- failure of pH, appearance or sub-visible particle criteria.

8.5 Data will be evaluated in accordance with ICH Q1E. For assay, CVD-oxide and total impurities, regression analysis with 95% one-sided confidence limits will be performed. Batch poolability will be assessed at the 0.25 significance level. Extrapolation beyond the period covered by real-time data will follow the limits given in ICH Q1E Appendix A.

## 9. Deviation handling

9.1 Any departure from this protocol must be recorded and assessed under SOP-QA-0105 (Deviation Management) before the affected data are used. The following events count as departures:

- missed or late pulls;
- chamber excursions;
- damaged or lost samples;
- use of reserve vials;
- testing outside the defined window.

The impact assessment must state whether the data remain valid for shelf-life evaluation.

9.2 Damaged samples may be replaced only with reserve vials from the same batch, condition and orientation. Replacement requires approval by the Stability Programme Lead and QA. The replacement must be documented on form FRM-QC-0219-02.

9.3 One deviation had been recorded against this study at the effective date of this version. Deviation DEV-2024-0219 recorded that two inverted 9-month vials of batch CV23B004 were broken in transit to the QC laboratory and were replaced with reserve vials from the same storage condition. The investigation attributed the breakage to an unsuitable transfer carrier. Samples are now moved between Room 3.14 and the QC laboratory in padded, compartmented carriers (part no. TC-STB-20), as required by SOP-QC-0231. The replacement vials had been stored inverted in chamber SC-05-01 for the full 9-month period. QA concluded that the 9-month data for batch CV23B004 are valid. The reserve inventory for the inverted 5°C condition of batch CV23B004 was recalculated and confirmed to be sufficient through 36 months.

9.4 Confirmed OOS results are reported to Regulatory Affairs within 3 working days. These results are assessed for notification to the competent authority.

## 10. Bracketing and matrixing

10.1 The study design follows ICH Q1D. Bracketing on fill volume is applied: the 4 mL and 20 mL presentations are tested, and the 10 mL presentation is covered because all three use the same Type I glass, stopper and solution. The three presentations share the same bulk solution composition and manufacturing process. They also share the same vial glass type and supplier, and the same 20 mm stopper formulation and coating. They differ only in fill volume and vial size.

10.2 The 4 mL presentation has the highest headspace-to-fill ratio and the highest ratio of stopper contact area to volume. It is therefore regarded as the worst case for oxidation (CVD-oxide formation) and for stopper interaction. The 20 mL presentation is the largest fill and is regarded as the worst case for sub-visible particle load per container.

10.3 Matrixing is not applied. Every batch, presentation, condition and orientation in the study design is tested at every time point listed in Section 7.

10.4 If the two bracketing presentations show different stability behaviour, the 10 mL presentation will be assigned the shorter of the two shelf lives. This applies unless supporting data are generated on 10 mL batches.

## 11. Post-approval commitments

11.1 The applicant commits to placing the first three commercial batches of each fill volume on long-term stability at 5°C ± 3°C through the approved shelf life. This commitment includes the 10 mL presentation, which is bracketed in the registration study.

11.2 These commitment batches will be tested according to the long-term schedule and acceptance criteria of this protocol. After the commitment is fulfilled, at least one commercial batch of each fill volume per year will enter the ongoing programme defined in STAB-009.

11.3 Registration stability will continue through 36 months for all six batches, even if a shelf life shorter than 36 months is initially approved. Updated data will be submitted as a variation to support any extension of the shelf life.
