# In-Use Stability Protocol for Tirexafen Oral Suspension After First Opening

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This protocol defines the design, conduct and evaluation of an in-use stability study for Tirexafen oral suspension 40 mg/mL after the bottle is first opened. The study simulates patient use of a multi-dose container under defined conditions.

1.2 The data generated will be used to establish and support the in-use shelf life stated in the product labelling. This statement covers the period during which an opened bottle remains within specification. The data will also confirm that the preservative system remains effective throughout that period.

1.3 The study design follows the principles of the EMA Note for Guidance on In-Use Stability Testing of Human Medicinal Products (CPMP/QWP/2934/99). It is supplementary to the registration stability programme described in STAB-004.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This protocol applies to Tirexafen oral suspension 40 mg/mL, filled in 150 mL Type III amber glass bottles. The bottles are closed with a 28 mm child-resistant polypropylene cap with an LDPE liner and a press-in bottle adaptor. Each bottle is supplied with a 5 mL graduated oral syringe.

2.2 The protocol covers drug product manufactured at the Ashby Road site to the commercial process and packaging configuration.

2.3 The following studies are outside the scope of this protocol:

- Long-term and accelerated stability studies on unopened containers. These are covered by STAB-004 at 25°C/60% RH and 40°C/75% RH, with 30°C/65% RH as the intermediate condition.
- Stability of product manufactured at the Riverton site, which is covered by STAB-005.

2.4 Release and shelf-life specifications for the product are defined in SPEC-B. Where this protocol applies in-use acceptance criteria that differ from the release limits in SPEC-B, the in-use criteria in Section 8 apply.

## 5. Analytical Methods

5.1 All testing is performed with validated methods. These are the same methods referenced in SPEC-B, except where stated otherwise.

| Test | Method number | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | AM-TXF-006 | Visual inspection against reference description |
| Resuspendability | AM-TXF-007 | Inversion count to homogeneity after 24 h standing |
| Assay of tirexafen | AM-TXF-001 | Reversed-phase HPLC-UV, 254 nm |
| Related substances (TXF-acid, unspecified impurities, total) | AM-TXF-001 | Reversed-phase HPLC-UV, 254 nm, gradient |
| Sodium benzoate content | AM-TXF-004 | Reversed-phase HPLC-UV, 225 nm |
| pH | AM-GEN-012 | Potentiometric, Ph. Eur. 2.2.3 |
| Viscosity | AM-TXF-009 | Rotational viscometer, spindle LV-2, 30 rpm, 25°C |
| Total aerobic microbial count (TAMC) | MB-007 | Plate count, Ph. Eur. 2.6.12 |
| Total combined yeasts and moulds count (TYMC) | MB-007 | Plate count, Ph. Eur. 2.6.12 |
| Absence of *Escherichia coli* | MB-008 | Ph. Eur. 2.6.13 |
| Preservative efficacy | MB-011 | Ph. Eur. 5.1.3, oral preparations criteria |

5.2 Samples for physicochemical testing are taken after the bottle has been resuspended in accordance with AM-TXF-007. Microbiological samples are taken aseptically in a Grade A unidirectional airflow cabinet before any other portion is removed from the bottle.

5.3 Results are reported to the number of decimal places stated in the acceptance criteria. Rounding follows SOP-QC-044.

## 6. Storage Conditions

6.1 In-use bottles are stored in walk-in stability chamber CH-B07. The chamber is qualified at 25°C/60% RH and mapped in accordance with SOP-FAC-102. It is monitored continuously by the site environmental monitoring system, with alarm limits set inside the tolerance band. The bottles are stored upright, in their cartons, protected from light.

6.2 Opened bottles will be stored at 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH and a 5 mL dose will be withdrawn twice daily with the supplied oral syringe to simulate patient use. Withdrawals take place on every calendar day, including weekends and public holidays. They are made at approximately 08:00 and 16:00, with at least six hours between doses.

6.3 **Withdrawal procedure.** At each withdrawal the Stability Technician performs the following steps:

1. Remove the bottle from the chamber.
2. Shake the bottle for 10 seconds.
3. Remove the cap.
4. Insert the syringe into the adaptor, invert the bottle and withdraw 5 mL.
5. Replace the cap and return the bottle to the chamber within 5 minutes.

<!-- page 4 -->

The time out of the chamber is recorded on Form F-STB-031.

6.4 **Volume management.** Withdrawn doses are discarded into a waste container until approximately 45 mL remains in the bottle. This retained volume is needed for end-of-period testing. From then until the bottle's assigned test point, each withdrawn 5 mL dose is expelled back into the bottle immediately. This draw-and-return approach maintains the full frequency of closure opening, syringe contact and headspace exchange.

6.5 **Unopened controls.** Two unopened control bottles are stored in CH-B07 alongside the in-use bottles and are tested at Day 30. This allows changes caused by repeated opening to be distinguished from changes caused by storage time alone.

6.6 **Chamber excursions.** Excursions outside the stated tolerance are handled in accordance with SOP-FAC-118 and Section 9 of this protocol.

## 7. Testing Schedule

7.1 In-use samples will be tested on days 0, 7, 14, 21 and 30 after first opening of the bottle. Each pull is made within ±1 day of the nominal time point. Testing starts within 2 working days of the pull. If a sample cannot be tested immediately, it is held at 2–8°C for physicochemical tests only. Microbiological samples are always tested on the day of the pull.

7.2 The planned Day 0 for batch B-TXF-22118 is 2024-06-10. The planned final pull is 2024-07-10, which is before the batch expiry date of 2024-08-31.

## 8. Acceptance Criteria

8.1 The in-use shelf life will be supported only if every individual bottle result, at every time point, meets the criteria below. Throughout the in-use period the suspension must meet assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, TXF-acid not more than 0.5%, pH between 4.5 and 5.5, sodium benzoate 80.0–110.0% of the labelled amount, and a total aerobic microbial count not more than 100 CFU/mL. The sodium benzoate limit is the shelf-life limit. It differs from the tighter release limit in SPEC-B, which does not apply to in-use samples.

| Test | In-use acceptance criterion |
|---|---|
| Appearance | White to off-white homogeneous suspension after shaking, with a characteristic orange odour |
| Resuspendability | Homogeneous within 20 inversions |
| Assay | 95.0–105.0% of label claim |
| TXF-acid | NMT 0.5% |
| Any unspecified impurity | NMT 0.20% |
| Total impurities | NMT 1.0% |
| pH | 4.5–5.5 |
| Sodium benzoate | 80.0–110.0% of labelled amount |
| Viscosity | 250–600 mPa·s (for information; trend only) |
| TAMC | NMT 100 CFU/mL |
| TYMC | NMT 10 CFU/mL |
| *E. coli* | Absent in 1 mL |
| Preservative efficacy | Meets Ph. Eur. 5.1.3 criteria for oral preparations |

8.2 Viscosity is reported for information only. A change of more than 20% from the Day 0 mean will be assessed in the study report. Such a change is not, by itself, a failure.

8.3 The data will be evaluated as described in ICH Q1E. Because the in-use period is short, no extrapolation beyond 30 days is permitted. The in-use period will be stated in labelling as "Use within 30 days of first opening" only if all criteria are met at Day 30.

## 9. Deviation Handling

9.1 Any departure from this protocol is recorded and assessed in accordance with SOP-QA-021 (Deviation Management). Examples include:

- a missed or late withdrawal;
- a pull made outside the ±1 day window;
- a chamber excursion;
- use of an incorrect syringe;
- a breach of aseptic technique during microbiological sampling.

The Stability Manager notifies QA within one working day of discovery.

9.2 Missed withdrawals are recorded on Form F-STB-031. They are not made up by withdrawing extra doses later.

<!-- page 6 -->

- If more than two withdrawals in total are missed on any bottle, that bottle is replaced from reserve stock. The replacement bottle restarts at Day 0, and its timeline is documented in the deviation record.
- A temperature or humidity excursion longer than 24 hours triggers an impact assessment before testing continues at the next time point.

9.3 Out-of-specification and out-of-trend results are investigated in accordance with SOP-QC-030. Retesting is not performed until the Phase I laboratory investigation is complete. Microbiological OOS results are investigated in accordance with SOP-MB-015, which includes identification of any isolate.

9.4 All deviations, together with their impact assessments and the investigations linked to them, are summarised in the final study report.

9.5 No deviations have been recorded under this protocol at the effective date of version 1.1.

## 10. Bracketing and Matrixing

10.1 Bracketing is not applicable to the in-use study; instead, one batch near the end of its shelf life is tested as the worst case for preservative efficacy. The product is registered in a single strength and a single container size. As a result, there are no extremes of strength, fill volume or container size to bracket as described in ICH Q1D.

10.2 The batch was chosen for the following reasons:

- Sodium benzoate content falls gradually during storage of unopened containers. The long-term data under STAB-004 show a decline of about 2% per year.
- Batch B-TXF-22118 at 22 months therefore represents the lowest preservative content expected at the point of dispensing.
- If the preservative system remains effective in this batch throughout 30 days of simulated use, a fresher batch can be expected to perform at least as well.

10.3 Matrixing is not applied. Every bottle assigned to a time point is tested for every scheduled attribute, as shown in Section 7.
