## 1. Purpose

1.1 This protocol defines the ongoing (post-approval) stability program for commercial batches of Velorastat film-coated tablets 25 mg, 50 mg and 100 mg packed in HDPE bottles with desiccant. The program confirms that commercial production continues to meet the registered shelf-life specification throughout the approved 36-month shelf life when the product is stored under the labelled conditions.

1.2 The program also meets the post-approval stability commitments in the marketing authorisation. It provides the data used in the annual Product Quality Review (PQR) and supports trend evaluation in accordance with ICH Q1E.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This protocol applies to commercial batches of Velorastat film-coated tablets manufactured at the Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals Harwick Manufacturing Site and packed in the registered HDPE bottle presentation (30 tablets per bottle).

2.2 The following are outside the scope of this protocol:

- Registration stability studies, which are governed by STAB-001.
- Stability studies supporting Alu/Alu blister packaging, which are governed by STAB-003.
- Stability studies for process validation, change control or deviation investigations. These are set up under separate study-specific protocols raised in accordance with SOP-QC-0412.

2.3 The registered drug product specification is SPEC-A. Where this protocol and SPEC-A differ, SPEC-A takes precedence. Any difference shall be raised through change control.

## 4. Materials and batches

4.1 Batch selection. Each calendar year in which Velorastat film-coated tablets are manufactured, the Stability Coordinator selects batches for enrolment in accordance with Section 10. Selected batches shall be representative commercial batches. Batches with open deviations affecting product quality shall not be selected unless QA approves their selection.

4.2 Batch numbering. Velorastat batch numbers follow the format VL + two-digit manufacturing campaign number + site code (A = Harwick) + three-digit sequence number. The campaign number is assigned sequentially and is not a calendar year.

4.3 Sample quantities. For each enrolled batch, 22 bottles of 30 tablets are taken from the finished-goods packaging line at the mid-point of packaging. They are allocated as follows:

| Use | Bottles |
|---|---|
| Initial (T0) testing | 4 |
| 12-month timepoint | 4 |
| 24-month timepoint | 4 |
| 36-month timepoint | 4 |
| Reserve (OOS/OOT investigation, retest) | 6 |
| Total per batch | 22 |

## 5. Analytical methods

5.1 All testing is performed using validated methods listed in SPEC-A. Methods are executed at the current effective version on the date of testing.

| Test | Method number | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | AM-VLR-001 | Visual inspection |
| Assay | AM-VLR-012 | RP-HPLC, UV 254 nm |
| Degradation products (VLR-ketone, unspecified, total) | AM-VLR-013 | RP-HPLC, UV 230 nm, gradient |
| Dissolution | AM-VLR-021 | USP Apparatus 2, 50 rpm, 900 mL pH 6.8 phosphate buffer; HPLC finish |
| Water content | AM-VLR-030 | Coulometric Karl Fischer |
| Hardness | AM-VLR-034 | Tablet hardness tester |
| Microbial enumeration (TAMC/TYMC) | AM-MIC-005 | Ph. Eur. 2.6.12 |
| Specified microorganisms (E. coli) | AM-MIC-006 | Ph. Eur. 2.6.13 |

<!-- page 3 -->

5.2 Methods AM-VLR-012 and AM-VLR-013 were shown to be stability-indicating during registration stability (STAB-001). This included forced degradation under acid, base, oxidative, thermal and photolytic stress. VLR-ketone is the principal oxidative degradant and is quantified against its qualified reference standard, RS-VLR-K-03.

5.3 A method change made during the program shall be bridged in accordance with SOP-QC-0315 before results generated with the new method are compared with earlier data.

## 6. Storage conditions

6.1 Samples are stored upright in their commercial container closure in qualified stability chambers. Chamber qualification and monitoring follow SOP-ENG-0217. The primary chamber for this program is SC-112 (walk-in, Building 4, Room 4.07). The designated backup chamber is SC-114, which is qualified at the same set points.

6.2 Annual commercial batches are stored only at 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH, as no accelerated testing is required for the ongoing program. The registration studies in STAB-001 included intermediate (30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH) and accelerated (40°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH) conditions, and photostability testing per ICH Q1B. None of these is repeated here. The STAB-003 blister study includes 30°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH storage, which likewise does not apply to the bottle presentation covered by this protocol.

6.3 Chamber excursions are handled in accordance with SOP-QC-0420. Excursions shorter than 24 hours and within ±3°C / ±7% RH are documented and assessed by the Stability Coordinator. Any other excursion requires a deviation.

## 7. Testing schedule

7.1 Each annual batch is tested at 0, 12, 24 and 36 months of storage. The T0 result is the initial stability timepoint. It is generated from the stability sample set and does not use the batch release result, although both are reported for comparison.

7.2 Pull window. Samples shall be withdrawn from the chamber, and testing shall start, within ±14 calendar days of the nominal timepoint date. Testing outside this window requires a deviation in accordance with Section 9.

7.3 The tests performed at each timepoint, and the tablets used for each test, are shown below. Pulled tablets that are not used are retained until the timepoint result is approved.

| Test | T0 | 12 M | 24 M | 36 M | Tablets per timepoint |

| Appearance | X | X | X | X | 20 |
| Assay | X | X | X | X | 10 (composite) |
| Degradation products | X | X | X | X | 10 (composite) |
| Dissolution | X | X | X | X | 6 (12 or 24 if S2/S3) |

## 8. Acceptance criteria

8.1 Results are evaluated against the registered shelf-life specification of assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, VLR-ketone not more than 0.3%, total impurities not more than 1.0%, and dissolution not less than 80% (Q) in 30 minutes. The remaining shelf-life tests in SPEC-A are evaluated as shown below. Hardness is a monitoring parameter only and has no acceptance criterion.

| Test | Shelf-life acceptance criterion (SPEC-A) |
|---|---|
| Appearance | Pink (25 mg), orange (50 mg) or brown (100 mg), round, biconvex, film-coated tablets, debossed with "VL" and strength |
| Assay | 95.0–105.0% of label claim |
| VLR-ketone | NMT 0.3% |
| Any unspecified degradation product | NMT 0.2% |
| Total impurities | NMT 1.0% |
| Dissolution | NLT 80% (Q) in 30 minutes |
| Water content | NMT 5.0% w/w |
| TAMC | NMT 10³ CFU/g |
| TYMC | NMT 10² CFU/g |
| E. coli | Absent in 1 g |
| Hardness | Report result |

8.2 Out-of-trend (OOT) evaluation. Assay, VLR-ketone, total impurities and water content are trended for each strength using the regression-based approach in SOP-QC-0433. Data from registration batches (STAB-001) form the reference population. A result outside the 95% prediction interval is treated as OOT and investigated even if it is within specification.

8.3 Out-of-specification (OOS) results are investigated in accordance with SOP-QC-0302. A confirmed OOS result at any timepoint is escalated to Regulatory Affairs within 2 working days, so that reportability and the impact on distributed batches can be assessed.

## 9. Deviation handling

9.1 Departures from this protocol are recorded and assessed in accordance with SOP-QA-0105 (Deviation Management). Examples include missed or late pulls, chamber excursions outside the limits in Section 6.3, sample mix-ups and testing with unapproved method versions. Each deviation includes an impact assessment that states whether the affected data may be used for shelf-life confirmation and trending.

9.2 Deviations raised against this protocol since the previous version are summarised below.

Deviation DEV-2024-0087 recorded that the 12-month samples of batch VL24A013 were tested 11 days after the end of the permitted pull window; the impact assessment concluded that the data remain valid. The nominal 12-month date was 2023-12-05 and the window closed on 2023-12-19. Samples remained in chamber SC-112 at the specified condition until withdrawal. The HPLC system assigned to AM-VLR-012/AM-VLR-013 was out of service during the holiday shutdown, and testing began on 2023-12-30. The assessment considered three points:

<!-- page 5 -->

- Chamber records showed no excursions during the extended storage period.
- An additional 11 days at 25°C / 60% RH is a small fraction of a 12-month interval. Under ICH Q1E, this does not materially affect the regression of time-dependent attributes.
- All results met the acceptance criteria in Section 8. VLR-ketone was 0.08% and assay was 99.6% of label claim, and both were consistent with the registration trend.

The corrective action, CAPA-2024-0019, requires backup HPLC capacity to be booked before planned shutdowns. It is tracked under SOP-QA-0110.

9.3 Late pulls should be avoided. Where a pull is late, the actual storage duration is recorded in LIMS, and that duration is used in statistical evaluation instead of the nominal time.

## 10. Bracketing and matrixing

10.1 The registration stability program in STAB-001 used a bracketing design under ICH Q1D. Velorastat 25 mg, 50 mg and 100 mg tablets share the following features:

- They are compressed from a common blend.
- They have the same proportions of excipients, differing only in tablet weight.
- They are packed in the same HDPE bottle and desiccant configuration.

Registration data showed comparable stability behaviour across all three strengths, so the 25 mg and 100 mg strengths were confirmed as valid extremes.

10.2 Consistent with the bracketing design in STAB-001, one batch each of the 25 mg and 100 mg strengths is enrolled per year and the 50 mg strength is represented by the brackets. If a bracketing strength shows an unfavourable trend or a confirmed OOS result, QC and QA shall assess whether to enrol a 50 mg batch the following year. This assessment is documented in the stability report.

10.3 No matrixing is applied in this program. All enrolled batches are tested at every timepoint in Section 7 for every scheduled test.

10.4 Any change to formulation, manufacturing process, batch size range or container closure that could affect the validity of the bracketing approach is assessed through change control (SOP-QA-0201). The assessment determines whether the bracketing design remains justified.

## 11. Post-approval commitments

11.1 As a post-approval commitment, at least one commercial batch of each bracketing strength will be added to the ongoing stability program in every year in which the product is manufactured. Regulatory Affairs records this commitment in the commitment register under reference PAC-VLR-02. The Head of QC confirms annually, as part of the PQR, that it has been met.

<!-- page 6 -->

11.2 In any calendar year in which a bracketing strength is not manufactured, no batch of that strength is enrolled. The reason is documented in the PQR. Enrolment resumes in the next year in which that strength is manufactured.

11.3 Stability data from this program are summarised in an annual stability report (template FRM-QC-0433-01). Each report covers:

- results by batch and timepoint;
- trend plots for assay, VLR-ketone, total impurities and water content;
- a list of deviations and OOT/OOS investigations;
- a conclusion on continued support for the 36-month shelf life.

The report is approved by QC and QA within 60 days of the PQR period end.

11.4 Batches enrolled under this program remain on study until the 36-month timepoint is complete, unless QA approves early termination through a documented rationale.
