# Drug Product Specification: Tirexafen Oral Suspension 40 mg/mL

**Document number:** SPEC-B
**Version:** 3.1
**Effective date:** 2024-11-18
**Status:** Current
**Document type:** Specification
**Owner:** Quality Control, Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals, Halsworth Site

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This specification defines the tests, analytical procedures and acceptance criteria for Tirexafen Oral Suspension 40 mg/mL (Product B). Batches must meet these criteria to be certified for release and to remain acceptable throughout the approved shelf life.

1.2 The specification is the reference against which Quality Control (QC) tests release and stability samples. The Qualified Person (QP) uses it, together with the batch record review, as the basis for batch certification.

1.3 This document also records the rationale for each acceptance criterion so that future changes can be assessed against the original justification.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This specification applies to all commercial and clinical batches of Tirexafen Oral Suspension 40 mg/mL filled into 150 mL Type III amber glass bottles. Each bottle is closed with a 28 mm child-resistant polypropylene cap with an LDPE liner. The specification covers product manufactured at the Aldencourt Halsworth site (primary site) and at the Riverton site (secondary site, approved under change control CC-2023-0417).

2.2 The specification applies to:

- release testing of finished product by Halsworth QC (Laboratory QC-2) or Riverton QC (Laboratory RQ-1);
- stability testing performed under STAB-004 (Registration Stability Protocol for Tirexafen Oral Suspension 40 mg/mL) and STAB-005 (Stability Protocol for Tirexafen Oral Suspension Manufactured at the Riverton Site);
- in-use stability testing performed under STAB-006 (In-Use Stability Protocol for Tirexafen Oral Suspension After First Opening), to the extent defined in that protocol.

2.3 This specification does not apply to the drug substance, excipients, bulk suspension held before filling, or packaging components. Separate raw material and component specifications in the RM- and PC- series cover those materials.

## 3. Product Description and Composition

3.1 **Description.** Tirexafen Oral Suspension 40 mg/mL is a white to off-white, homogeneous, orange-flavoured suspension. It may settle on standing and readily redisperses on gentle shaking. Each bottle contains a nominal 150 mL with a target fill of 153 mL, which provides 6.0 g of tirexafen. A 10 mL polypropylene oral dosing syringe with 0.5 mL graduations is co-packed in the carton.

3.2 **Dosing.** The maximum recommended daily dose for adults is 400 mg tirexafen (10 mL) given as two divided doses. This dose determines the impurity reporting, identification and qualification thresholds applied in Section 5.

## 5. Justification of Specification

5.1 **General.** The specification follows ICH Q6A and ICH Q3B(R2). It is based on data from three primary registration batches manufactured at Halsworth (B-22014, B-22015 and B-22016, 600 L scale) and stored under STAB-004. It is also based on three Riverton batches (R-23101, R-23102 and R-23103, 600 L scale) stored under STAB-005. Registration samples were stored in chambers SC-H04 (25 °C/60% RH), SC-H05 (30 °C/65% RH) and SC-H07 (40 °C/75% RH) at Halsworth, and in chambers RSC-02 and RSC-03 at Riverton. Long-term data are available to 36 months for the Halsworth batches and to 18 months for the Riverton batches. These data support a shelf life of 24 months when stored below 25 °C, followed by an in-use period of 28 days after first opening.

5.2 **Shelf-life criteria.** The shelf-life acceptance criteria for Tirexafen oral suspension 40 mg/mL are assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, TXF-acid not more than 0.5%, total impurities not more than 1.5%, pH 4.5 to 5.5, and sodium benzoate content 80.0–110.0% of the labelled amount. These criteria, together with the other shelf-life limits in Section 4, apply to all stability time points under STAB-004 and STAB-005 and to in-use samples tested under STAB-006.

5.3 **Assay.** Release results for the six registration batches ranged from 99.1% to 101.4% of label claim. The mean decrease at 24 months at 25 °C/60% RH was 1.6%, and the largest decrease observed at 40 °C/75% RH after 6 months was 3.2%. The release range of 97.0–103.0% covers manufacturing variability, including a fill-weight and suspension homogeneity contribution estimated at ±1.2% from process validation. It also covers analytical variability, with intermediate precision RSD of 0.6%. The release range leaves enough margin that a batch released at the lower release limit will remain within the wider shelf-life range at expiry.

5.4 **TXF-acid.** TXF-acid forms by hydrolysis of the tirexafen amide linkage and is the main degradation product. Release levels ranged from 0.05% to 0.11%. At 24 months under long-term conditions, the highest level was 0.31% (batch B-22015), with a mean increase of 0.009% per month. Projections based on regression analysis per ICH Q1E, using the upper 95% confidence bound, predict a maximum of 0.38% at 24 months. The shelf-life limit of NMT 0.5% is above the ICH Q3B(R2) qualification threshold of 0.2% for a maximum daily dose of 400 mg. This level has been qualified in a 13-week oral repeat-dose rat toxicity study (study report TOX-TXF-118), in which TXF-acid was administered at levels equivalent to 2.4% relative to tirexafen. TXF-acid is also a known human metabolite of tirexafen. The release limit of NMT 0.3% ensures compliance with the shelf-life limit at expiry.

<!-- page 4 -->

5.5 **TXF-N-oxide.** TXF-N-oxide is an oxidative degradant seen mainly at accelerated conditions; it reached 0.12% after 6 months at 40 °C/75% RH. Under long-term conditions it did not exceed 0.09% at 36 months. The shelf-life limit of NMT 0.3% has been qualified in study TOX-TXF-118.

5.6 **Unspecified degradation products.** For a maximum daily dose of 400 mg, ICH Q3B(R2) sets a reporting threshold of 0.1%, an identification threshold of 0.2% and a qualification threshold of 0.2%. The shelf-life limit of NMT 0.20% for any unspecified degradation product matches the identification threshold. The reporting threshold of 0.05% applied in AM-TXF-001 is a voluntary tightening that supports trend analysis. No unspecified degradation product exceeded 0.08% in any registration batch.

5.7 **Total impurities.** Total impurities at release ranged from 0.21% to 0.36%. The highest result at 24 months under long-term conditions was 0.71%, and the highest at 6 months accelerated was 1.08%. The shelf-life limit of NMT 1.5% allows for the combined maximum of the specified and unspecified limits that can realistically occur together. The release limit of NMT 1.0% is set accordingly.

5.8 **Sodium benzoate content.** Sodium benzoate preserves the product, and its efficacy depends on the undissociated benzoic acid fraction, which is controlled by pH. Content falls slowly through adsorption onto the cellulose suspending system and through migration into the cap liner. The mean loss was 5.8% at 24 months at 25 °C/60% RH, and the largest single-batch loss was 8.4%. Preservative efficacy testing was performed on a batch deliberately manufactured at 80% of the labelled sodium benzoate amount (development batch D-21-PE80). This batch met the Ph. Eur. 5.1.3 criteria for oral preparations, which supports the lower shelf-life limit of 80.0%. Preservative efficacy testing is also performed at the 24-month time point and at the end of the 28-day in-use period under STAB-006.

5.9 **pH.** The citrate buffer is designed to hold the pH near 5.0. At this pH, enough sodium benzoate is present in the undissociated form for preservative activity, and tirexafen hydrolysis rates are close to their minimum according to the pH-rate profile in development report DEV-TXF-031. Release results ranged from 4.86 to 5.07, with a maximum drift of −0.14 units over 36 months. The release range is 4.7–5.3 and the wider shelf-life range is 4.5–5.5.

5.10 **Physical attributes.** The viscosity, redispersibility and particle size limits are based on development data showing that dose uniformity from the syringe is maintained within these ranges (DEV-TXF-044). Particle size growth by Ostwald ripening was limited, with a maximum D90 increase of 3.1 µm at 24 months. The dissolution criterion is discriminating for D90 values above 35 µm.

5.11 **Microbiological quality.** The microbial limits follow Ph. Eur. 5.1.4 for non-sterile aqueous preparations for oral use.

5.12 **Tests not included.** Residual solvents are controlled at the drug substance and excipient level per ICH Q3C, and no solvents are used in drug product manufacture. Elemental impurities were assessed per ICH Q3D in risk assessment RA-TXF-007, which found all elements below 30% of the PDE, so routine testing is not required.
