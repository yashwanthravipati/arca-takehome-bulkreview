# STAB-012 Ongoing Stability Program for Moxilurine 150 mg Hard Capsules

| Document number | Version | Effective date | Status | Supersedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STAB-012 | 1.3 | 2024-08-26 | Current | STAB-012 version 1.2 |

Owning department: Quality Control, Stability Unit, Aldencourt Pharmaceuticals, Brenmoor Site, Building 4.

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines the ongoing stability program for Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules (Product D) manufactured and packaged at the Aldencourt Brenmoor site. The program confirms that commercial batches continue to meet the registered shelf-life specification throughout the approved 36-month shelf life when stored under the registered conditions. It also detects any drift in the stability profile that could arise from changes in raw materials, equipment, process parameters or packaging components over the commercial lifecycle of the product.

The program is a routine Good Manufacturing Practice activity. It generates data that are trended annually and reported in the Product Quality Review for Product D. The program does not replace the registration stability data generated under STAB-010, which remain the basis for the approved shelf life and storage statement.

## 2. Scope

This protocol applies to commercial batches of Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules filled into hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC) shells from the primary qualified shell supplier and packaged in the following registered presentations:

- PVC/PVDC (250 µm PVC / 60 g/m² PVDC) blister sealed with 20 µm hard-temper aluminium lidding foil, 10 capsules per blister card
- 30-count HDPE bottle with induction-sealed polypropylene child-resistant closure
- 60-count HDPE bottle with induction-sealed polypropylene child-resistant closure
- 90-count HDPE bottle with induction-sealed polypropylene child-resistant closure

Batches filled into HPMC shells from the alternate supplier are evaluated separately under STAB-011 and are outside the scope of this protocol. They are not eligible for enrollment here until the alternate shell source has been formally included through change control. This protocol does not cover development, registration, or change-driven stability studies. Those studies are governed by STAB-010, STAB-011, or study-specific protocols raised under change control.

The approved shelf life is 36 months. The registered storage statement is "Do not store above 30°C. Store in the original package in order to protect from moisture."

## 4. Materials and Batches

### 4.1 Batch selection

One commercial batch of Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules is enrolled each calendar year. The batch is selected from the first quarter's production of the presentation scheduled for that year, as defined in Section 10.

To be eligible, a batch must:

- have been released against SPEC-D;
- have been manufactured with the registered commercial process and batch size (450,000 capsules); and
- have no open deviation that could plausibly affect stability.

If no eligible batch of the scheduled presentation is packaged in the first quarter, the Stability Coordinator enrolls the first eligible batch packaged later in the same calendar year and records the reason in the study record.

## 5. Analytical Methods

All methods are validated in accordance with ICH Q2(R1). Before release for routine use, each method was shown to be stability-indicating, or was verified as fit for purpose for stability testing.

| Test | Method number | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| Description | AM-D-100 | Visual examination of capsule shell and contents |
| Identification | AM-D-101 | HPLC retention time comparison with Moxilurine reference standard |
| Assay | AM-D-101 | Reversed-phase HPLC, UV detection at 272 nm, external standard |
| Related substances (MXL-dimer, MXL-hydroxy acid, unspecified, total) | AM-D-102 | Reversed-phase gradient HPLC, UV detection at 230 nm, relative response factors applied |
| Dissolution | AM-D-103 | Ph. Eur./USP Apparatus 2 (paddle) with sinkers. 50 rpm, 900 mL 0.1 N HCl at 37.0 ± 0.5°C. HPLC finish per AM-D-101 |
| Water content | AM-D-104 | Coulometric Karl Fischer titration of capsule contents |
| Microbial enumeration (TAMC, TYMC, E. coli) | AM-GEN-210 | Ph. Eur. 2.6.12 and 2.6.13 / USP <61> and <62> |

Reference standard lots are used and qualified in accordance with SOP-QC-031. Method revisions issued after the effective date of this protocol may be applied without revising the protocol, provided change control confirms equivalence. The method revision used is recorded with each result in LIMS.

## 6. Storage Conditions

Ongoing stability batches are stored at 30°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH, matching the registered long-term condition. This condition corresponds to Climatic Zone IVb. Data at this condition support the registered storage statement in all markets in which Moxilurine 150 mg hard capsules are supplied.

The registration study described in STAB-010 also included 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH, 30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH, and accelerated storage at 40°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH. Those conditions are not used in this ongoing program, and no intermediate or accelerated samples are placed on storage under this protocol.

Samples are stored in walk-in chamber STC-07 in Building 4, Room 4.112. Chamber STC-09 in Room 4.114 is qualified as the backup chamber at the same set point. Both chambers are:

- mapped annually;
- calibrated against traceable reference probes; and
- monitored continuously by the site environmental monitoring system, with temperature and humidity recorded at 5-minute intervals.

<!-- page 4 -->

Alarms are set at ± 1.5°C and ± 4% RH from set point. Out-of-tolerance excursions are managed according to SOP-QC-045 and Section 9 of this protocol.

If samples must be transferred to STC-09, the transfer is recorded in the chamber logbook and in LIMS. Transfer time outside a qualified chamber must not exceed 2 hours.

## 7. Testing Schedule

Each enrolled batch is tested at 0, 12, 24 and 36 months. The 0-month result is generated on samples taken from the packaging line and tested within 30 days of packaging. Release results are not substituted for the 0-month stability result.

The allowed pull window is ± 14 days of the nominal date for every pull after 0 months. Analysis must start within 30 days of the pull. Samples awaiting analysis are returned to the storage chamber until testing begins.

The Stability Coordinator generates the pull calendar from LIMS at enrollment. Each month, the calendar is cross-checked against the physical sample inventory in STC-07. A study ends after the 36-month pull has been tested, the results have been reviewed, and the data have been included in the annual stability summary. Remaining reserve samples are then destroyed according to SOP-QC-049.

## 8. Acceptance Criteria

Results are assessed against the shelf-life limits in SPEC-D. Batches are evaluated against the registered limits of assay 95.0–105.0% of label claim, MXL-dimer not more than 0.2%, total impurities not more than 1.0%, and dissolution not less than 75% (Q) in 45 minutes.

SPEC-D also contains limits for other attributes. These are applied in the same way and are listed in full below.

| Test | Shelf-life acceptance criterion |
|---|---|
| Description | White opaque HPMC capsule, size 1, printed "ALD D150" in black; contents white to off-white powder |
| Identification | Retention time of principal peak corresponds to reference standard (± 2%) (0 months only) |
| Assay | 95.0–105.0% of label claim |
| MXL-dimer | NMT 0.2% |
| MXL-hydroxy acid | NMT 0.3% |
| Any unspecified impurity | NMT 0.10% |
| Total impurities | NMT 1.0% |
| Dissolution | NLT 75% (Q) in 45 minutes |
| Water content | NMT 6.0% w/w |
| TAMC | NMT 10³ CFU/g |
| TYMC | NMT 10² CFU/g |
| Escherichia coli | Absent in 1 g |

<!-- page 5 -->

The release specification in SPEC-D has a narrower assay range than the shelf-life range above, and it is not applied to stability results.

Results are trended according to SOP-QC-063, using the regression and poolability approach described in ICH Q1E. A result is classified as out-of-trend (OOT) if it falls outside the 95% prediction interval derived from historical batches on the program. A result is also classified as OOT if the change from the previous timepoint exceeds the alert thresholds in SOP-QC-063. For MXL-dimer, that alert threshold is an increase of more than 0.05% between consecutive timepoints.

## 9. Deviation Handling

Any departure from this protocol is recorded as a deviation in the site quality management system according to SOP-QA-018. Examples include:

- a missed or late pull;
- testing outside the pull-to-test window;
- use of an unapproved method revision;
- a chamber excursion outside the qualified range; or
- loss or damage of samples.

The Stability Coordinator raises the deviation within one working day of discovery. Quality Assurance classifies it as minor, major or critical, based on its potential impact on the validity of the stability data.

For chamber excursions, Facilities and Engineering provides the excursion profile, including duration, peak values and mean kinetic temperature. The Stability Unit then assesses the impact on product. Excursions lasting less than 24 hours and staying within ± 5°C and ± 10% RH of set point are normally assessed as having no impact, and the supporting rationale is documented. Longer or larger excursions need a documented scientific justification, which may include reference to the accelerated data generated under STAB-010.

An out-of-specification (OOS) result is investigated according to SOP-QC-062. A confirmed OOS result on a marketed batch is escalated immediately to the Head of Quality and to Regulatory Affairs, so that the impact on distributed product can be assessed. OOT results are investigated according to SOP-QC-063.

All deviations, OOS and OOT investigations are summarized in the annual stability summary.

No deviations have been recorded under this protocol as of the effective date of version 1.3.

## 10. Bracketing and Matrixing

Bracketing is applied to the packaging presentations in line with ICH Q1D. All four presentations share the same capsule formulation, fill weight and HPMC shell, and the three bottle sizes share the same HDPE resin, wall thickness and closure system. The PVC/PVDC blister and the 90-count HDPE bottle are enrolled in alternate years, since the registration bracketing study showed the 90-count bottle to be the least protective bottle presentation.

<!-- page 6 -->

This finding is consistent with the higher moisture uptake per capsule observed in the 90-count bottle during STAB-010. It is attributed to the repeated in-use opening simulation and to the larger permeable surface area of that bottle. The 30-count and 60-count bottles are therefore represented by the 90-count bottle and are not enrolled separately.

The blister is a different moisture barrier system, so it is enrolled in its own right. Under the alternating scheme:

- odd-numbered years enroll the blister; and
- even-numbered years enroll the 90-count bottle.

Section 4.2 shows the batches enrolled so far under this scheme.

If annual trending shows a meaningful difference in water content, dissolution or degradant formation between the presentations, Quality Assurance and Regulatory Affairs will reassess the bracketing approach under change control. The same reassessment applies if the HDPE bottle or closure components change.

Matrixing is not applied. Every enrolled batch is tested for every attribute in Section 7 at each scheduled timepoint.
